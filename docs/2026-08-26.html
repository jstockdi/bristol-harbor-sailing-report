Bristol Harbor · Rhode Island

Wed 26 Aug 2026 · 18:00–20:00

Race window 18:00–20:00

Light-moderate S/SSE sea breeze (6–8 kt), veering toward the SSW; a building flood sets NNE up-bay through the window; high water mid-race — no depth worries.

Wind

A soft-to-moderate southerly evening breeze: race-window averages run 6–8 kt with gusts to 11–14 kt. Direction sits S/SSE (≈168–185°) and veers modestly through the window — clearest at the seaward East Passage fix, which backs-and-fills across ~13° and veers toward the SSW as the sea breeze matures.

The pressure gradient is the story. The strongest air is seaward and along the western edge — East Passage / off Hope Island (7.7 kt, gusts ~14) and off Poppasquash Point (7.4 kt) — while the harbor mouth at Hog Island Shoal (6.0 kt) and inner harbor (6.5 kt) are 1–2 kt softer. That's the familiar summer signal: more breeze the farther S/seaward you work, a softer patch up inside and in the lee to the E/SE of Poppasquash Neck.

Confidence is good — model spread is tight (0.3–2.0 kt across fixes) and wind_check has obs and model broadly agreeing (ratio 1.13). The live picture is consistent: Newport at the Passage mouth read SW ~216° at 7.4 kt avg (peak 11), already veered and sea-breezy, while Conimicut up-bay held SSE ~152° at 6.6 kt — expect that veer to work its way down the bay as the evening breeze fills.

Current

Both stations are on the flood through the entire window, setting up-bay (NNE, into the harbor):

  • Harbor mouth (Hog Island NW, ACT2171): flood begins 18:27 and builds to next slack at 20:52 — a gentle ~0.38 kt NNE (~011°) set, near-slack early in the window and never strong.
  • East Passage (Dyer Island W, ACT2156): flood from 17:45, peaking around 0.75–0.87 kt NNE (~023°), slack at 20:29 — materially stronger than inside, as always in the channel.

So a boat heading N/up-bay carries fair current, strongest in the Passage channel; a boat pushing S/seaward noses into it. The flow is weak at the mouth but real out in the Passage.

Tide

High water 19:35 at 3.73 ft lands square in the middle of the window — the previous low (0.33 ft) was back at 12:48. With a full, still-rising-then-cresting tide, no shallow-water caution for marks or committee-boat depth around the harbor edges tonight.

Tactical call

Course-agnostic: Two levers point the same way tonight — pressure lives S/seaward (East Passage, off Poppasquash) and fair flood favours the N/up-bay boat. When the course lets you choose, work toward the seaward breeze for the extra 1–2 kt and cleaner air, and avoid parking in the soft harbor-mouth/inner pocket or the lee to the E/SE of Poppasquash Neck. On any seaward leg, the flood sets NNE up-bay — heading N you get a lift/push; heading S you're pinched into it, so lay the up-bay marks with the current under you and stay off the mid-Passage channel axis when clawing seaward. Keep your head out for the veer toward the SSW and shift with it rather than committing early to one geographic edge.

If the course is set: - N–S windward/leeward: the E–W pressure gradient is the cross-course lever — the western/seaward edge (Poppasquash, toward the Passage) holds more breeze; ride the NNE flood on the up-bay (N) legs. - J-hook out of the harbor: start in the near-slack inner harbor, then the hook into the mouth/Passage puts you in the stronger flood — expect the favoured edge to flip on the turn, and use the fair up-bay set once you round seaward and head back N. - Around Hog Island: flood in every quadrant tonight (weak at the mouth, ~0.75 kt in the Passage) — call each leg by compass; the N/up-bay legs get the push, the S/seaward legs fight it, so protect the inside on the down-current turns. - Out-and-back (light air): pressure dominates — get S to the East Passage breeze first; the light flood is a secondary, mostly-helpful factor on the return N.
