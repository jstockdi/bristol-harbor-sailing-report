Bristol Harbor · Rhode Island

Wed 09 Sep 2026 · Daysail

Daysail 10:00–18:00

A lovely early-fall daysail — light and glassy this morning, a pleasant sea breeze filling in the afternoon; best sailing 1–5 PM.

The day

This is a genuinely nice day to get out — mellow enough for a relaxed family sail but with enough breeze in the afternoon to keep it fun. The morning starts soft and glassy (the anemometers are reading just 1–3 kt right now), then a gentle sea breeze builds through midday and settles into a steady 8–10 kt from the south. Warm, mostly sunny, and almost no rain risk — about as easy as September gets. The only thing to plan around is a very low midday tide, so mind the shallows.

Wind

Right now it's a drifter — Newport is showing around 7 kt from the S but Conimicut up-bay is nearly calm, so the harbor itself is still waking up. Expect the breeze to fill through late morning and into the early afternoon, swinging from the ESE/SE around to a classic southerly as the sea breeze establishes itself. Once it's in, you'll have a comfortable, powered-up 8–10 kt with occasional puffs — the models flag gusts pushing into the high teens/low 20s, so there'll be moments where a smaller boat gets a nice shove. Nothing reef-worthy, just enough to keep a hand on the sheet.

The forecast has been slightly over-blowing the light morning, so don't be surprised if it's slower to build than the numbers suggest — patience early pays off. The best window is roughly 1–5 PM, when the southerly is steadiest and strongest. Expect a bit more pressure out toward Hog Island and the East Passage, and a soft, patchy spot tucked in under the western Poppasquash shore — if you find yourself parked there, nudging out toward open water will usually find you moving again.

Tide & current

High water was early (06:52) with the next high at 19:20; the catch today is a very low midday low around 12:37 — essentially bottom-of-the-tide, near zero. Give the harbor edges, shoal spots off Poppasquash Point, and any skinny water near Hog Island Shoal a wide berth around midday; keep to charted depth and you'll be fine. Current in the harbor itself is gentle — it's ebbing (running SSW, seaward) through late morning, goes slack around 12:20, then turns to a mild flood (NNE, back up into the harbor) in the late afternoon. Out in the East Passage the flow is a bit livelier, easing to slack near 1:30 PM. With the southerly breeze building against that morning ebb you may notice a little short chop near the harbor mouth before midday — nothing alarming, just a heads-up so it doesn't catch you off guard.

Good to know

A beautiful early-fall day: highs of 72–76°F, partly-to-mostly sunny skies, and only an 8% chance of rain — no thunderstorm concern. It'll feel warm in the sun but pleasant on the water, so bring a layer for the afternoon breeze and sun protection for the bright stretches. If you want the easiest, warmest sail, head out around midday and enjoy the sea breeze filling in; aim to be back before the light fades in the evening. With those afternoon puffs into the high teens, it's a good day to have life jackets on the kids (and handy for everyone) — nothing rough, just smart sailing. Have a great day out there.
