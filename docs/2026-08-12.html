Bristol Harbor · Rhode Island

Wed 12 Aug 2026 · 18:00–20:00

Race window 18:00–20:00

Light WSW gradient breeze, 2–3 kt more pressure seaward; a building flood sets NNE into the harbor through the finish, and high water at 20:26 means no shoal worries.

Wind

A soft westerly evening, not a classic sea breeze. Race-window average runs 5–8 kt from the WSW (~248–264°) with gusts poking to 13–14 kt on the seaward fixes. The models are tightly stacked — spread under 1.5 kt at every fix — so treat the strength as reliably light-to-moderate, and the wind_check has obs and model within 5% (ratio 0.95), broadly agreeing.

The clear signal is the pressure gradient: inner harbor only ~5.3 kt, off Poppasquash ~5.7, but 7.3 kt at the Hog Island mouth and 7.6 kt out in the East Passage — a solid 2–3 kt more breeze the farther S/seaward you get. Direction backs ~10–12° over the window, from W-ish (~264°) toward the WSW (~250°). Live anemometers are light and swinging in the afternoon transition — Newport NW at ~4–7 kt, Conimicut SE and lighter — consistent with a slack, thermally-mixed evening that fills modestly from the WSW. With a westerly in the harbor, expect a soft patch and a veer (toward the E/S) in the lee to the E/SE of Poppasquash Neck as you clear its shadow.

Current

The window sits entirely on a building flood — set NNE up-bay, into the harbor. At the harbor mouth (Hog Island NW) slack was 13:23; the flood then builds to ~0.37 kt at 18:58 (set ~011°) and runs to the next slack at 21:25. Out in the East Passage (Dyer Island W) the flood is materially stronger — slack at 14:35, then ~0.74 kt at 18:16 (set ~023°), easing to slack at 21:02. So the whole race is flood: gentle inside the harbor, roughly twice as strong on the Passage channel axis.

Tide

Low was at 13:33 (−0.17 ft); the tide is rising all through the window toward high at 20:26 (4.68 ft). Depth is a non-issue — you'll have the most water of the day near the finish, so no shoal or committee-boat-depth cautions on the harbor edges tonight.

Tactical

Pressure is the first lever, current the second. With the WSW breeze 2–3 kt stronger seaward, the paying move is to work toward the S — the Hog Island mouth and East Passage — whenever the course lets you, and to avoid parking in the light air up inside the harbor and under the E/SE lee of Poppasquash Neck. Play the WSW backing trend in phase; it's shifty light air, so bank the leftward (toward-SSW) swings rather than committing early to one shore.

On the flood, current helps anyone heading N/up-bay — ride the stronger Passage channel flow for a NNE push toward the harbor. Heading S/seaward you're pushing into ~0.4–0.7 kt of foul flood, so hug the shallower shore edges out of the mid-channel set to duck the worst of it. The flow only builds through the window, so the seaward-vs-current trade sharpens toward 20:00.

If the course is set: - N–S windward/leeward — the E–W pressure gradient is your cross-course lever; favor the seaward (S) end for breeze, and note the flood pushes you N (helping toward a northern mark, foul toward a southern one). - J-hook out of the harbor — you start in near-slack, light inner-harbor air, then hook into stronger breeze and a firmer NNE flood at the mouth; expect the favored edge to flip on the turn — more pressure and more current once you're out past Hog. - Around Hog Island — call each leg on its own: seaward legs get the breeze but foul flood (work the shore edges), up-bay legs get the NNE current assist through the mouth and Passage. - Out-and-back (light air) — pure pressure play: get S to the East Passage breeze; the flood is secondary but favors the return leg home.
