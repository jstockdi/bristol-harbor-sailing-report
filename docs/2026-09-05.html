Bristol Harbor · Rhode Island

Sat 05 Sep 2026 · Daysail

Daysail 10:00–18:00

A pleasant, breezy-cool early-fall daysail — steady northerly 8-ish kt with mid-teen puffs; best from late morning through mid-afternoon.

The day

This is a lovely one for getting out — a mellow-to-lively daysail with real breeze but nothing intimidating. Expect a steady northerly around 8 knots with occasional puffs into the mid-teens, sunny skies, and crisp early-September air in the upper 60s to low 70s. It's a touch shiftier than a classic summer sea breeze (northerlies roll off the land and come in puffs), so you'll be trimming and adjusting rather than set-and-forget, but it's honest, sailable wind for anyone comfortable in a light chop.

Wind

The breeze is out of the north, slowly clocking toward the NNE as the day goes on. It's already up this morning — the up-bay anemometer at Conimicut is reading a solid ~12 kt, while the Newport station down at the East Passage mouth is softer near 7 kt, so you'll find a bit more push the farther north/up-bay you sail and a lighter, gustier feel down toward Hog Island and the Passage. Overall figure a comfortable 8 knots with puffs to 14–15 — nice sailing, powered-up in the gusts but not reef-it territory. The models actually ran a hair strong versus what the water's showing this morning, so if anything take the forecast as a soft ceiling. Being a northerly, expect the classic soft patch tucked in behind Poppasquash Neck on its east/southeast side — if you drift into a hole there, just nose back out toward open water to find pressure. The whole late-morning-through-mid-afternoon stretch is your sweet spot.

Tide & current

Low water is early, at 07:48 (about half a foot — very low), then the tide fills all day to a 3.9 ft high at 15:11 before ebbing again. That means for your whole daysail the water is rising and generous — good news for the shallow edges near the harbor fringes and Poppasquash shoals, though give the very shallowest spots a wide berth if you head out right at the start. Current inside the harbor is gentle; the flow you'll notice is out toward the harbor mouth at Hog Island and into the East Passage, where it runs harder. A midday flood sets up-bay (toward the NNE) and turns to slack around 15:40–16:00, then ebbs SSW/seaward later in the afternoon. With the wind out of the north opposing that up-bay flood midday, you may feel a little short, bumpy chop right in the entrance gut off Hog Island — nothing worrying, just a heads-up so it doesn't surprise you.

Good to know

Beautiful early-fall day: sunny to mostly sunny, highs only around 70–72°F with a cool morning near 66 — bring a layer, especially with a northerly breeze that'll feel brisk on the water. There's just a slight (≤30%) chance of a passing shower later in the day, so glance at the sky in the afternoon but no real thunderstorm concern. With mid-teen gusts around, it's a sensible life-jackets-on day for kids and less-experienced crew. A good plan: head out mid-to-late morning to catch the steady breeze and rising water, and aim to be back by late afternoon — both to stay ahead of any stray shower and to be in before the wind and current soften and swap in the early evening. Enjoy it!
