Bristol Harbor · Rhode Island

Thu 06 Aug 2026 · Daysail

Daysail 10:00–18:00

A lovely steady-southerly day — 9–10 kt sea breeze with gusts to the mid-teens; afternoon is prime.

The day

This is a really nice one to be on the water — a proper mellow-to-spirited summer daysail. A steady southerly of around 9–10 knots fills the harbor and the waters around Hog Island, with occasional gusts nudging the mid-teens. That's easy, comfortable sailing for just about everyone: enough breeze to move along and lean into, not so much that you're wrestling the boat. Sunny skies and warm air round it out. Go enjoy it.

Wind

Expect a classic S/SSW breeze all day, already blowing 6–9 kt at the docks by mid-morning and building into a friendly 9–10 kt through the afternoon, gusting to about 15–16 in the puffs. This is nice-sailing to lightly-powered-up territory — a single reef isn't needed for most boats, but it's fun, honest breeze. The best window is roughly noon to 5 PM, when the sea breeze is most settled and even.

Pressure is pretty consistent everywhere, with a touch more air out toward Hog Island and the East Passage than tucked up inside the harbor. The one soft spot to expect is the usual lee behind Poppasquash Neck (the west shore) — if you sail in close there on this southerly you'll find lighter, patchier air, so don't be surprised by a lull, and you'll pick the breeze back up as you clear the point. The live anemometers this morning (Newport ~6–7 kt SSW, Conimicut ~9–11 kt S) line up well with the forecast, so confidence is good.

Tide & current

High water is at 2:20 PM (3.9 ft) and the nearest lows fall outside your day — 6:59 AM before you launch and 8:33 PM after you're in — so you'll have plenty of water under you all afternoon. Still, give the shallow harbor edges and shoals off the island tips a respectful berth around the margins of the day.

For current: the tide is flooding up-bay (to the NNE, into the harbor) through midday, going slack around 3–3:30 PM, then ebbing seaward (to the SSW) after ~5 PM. The strongest flow is through the entrance gut at Hog Island Shoal and out in the East Passage channel (up to ~1 kt there) — near-slack inside the harbor itself. One friendly heads-up: with a southerly wind blowing against that midday up-bay flood, you may notice a bit of short, bouncy chop at the harbor mouth and in the Passage — nothing alarming, just wind-against-tide. It'll smooth out as the current eases toward mid-afternoon.

Good to know

Gorgeous summer conditions: 78–83°F, mostly sunny with just a passing morning cloud, and only about a 10% chance of a shower — essentially a dry, bright day. Bring sun protection: hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, and plenty of water, because that afternoon sun and heat add up out there.

A great plan is to head out around late morning to noon and ride the building breeze through the afternoon, aiming to be back by 6 PM or so as the ebb picks up and the day cools. With gusts reaching the mid-teens, it's a sensible day to keep life jackets on — especially for kids and less-experienced crew — and to keep a hand for the boat in the puffs. Otherwise, this is about as good as a Bristol summer daysail gets. Enjoy it!
