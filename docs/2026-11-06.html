Bristol Harbor · Rhode Island

Fri 06 Nov 2026 · Daysail

Daysail 10:00–18:00

Honestly a stay-ashore day — rain, thick fog and gusty south wind make it more hassle than fun.

The day

This one's marginal, and not in a borderline-exciting way — it's the wet-and-murky kind. You've got a mild November day (low 60s), but the sky is throwing heavy rain, patchy fog, and even a chance of thunderstorms, with visibility dropping to a mile or less. Add a south wind that's already gusting into the high teens and you've got conditions that are more about keeping track of other boats you can't see than enjoying a sail. If you're itching to go, a short, well-bundled poke around the inner harbor at slack water is the most you'd want — otherwise this is a good day for boat chores and a warm drink.

Wind

The breeze is up and from the south, funneling straight up the East Passage. Live readings tell the story: Newport at the Passage mouth is around 11 knots, but Conimicut up the bay is already honking at 18–19 knots gusting 20. The forecast averages look softer (6–8 knots), but the gusts tell the truth — expect powered-up sailing with real reef-it puffs to 20, especially the farther south and seaward you go. It'll be lighter and patchier inside the harbor, and you'll find the usual soft, shifty pocket tucking in behind Poppasquash Neck on the west shore. There's no clean "best window" today — the wind stays up and the weather stays poor right through the afternoon. If you must go, the calmest water is the protected inner harbor; anything out toward Hog Island and the Passage means full breeze and chop.

Tide & current

Low water lands at 11:28 AM and it's a very low one — just 0.3 ft — so steer well clear of the shallow edges and skinny spots near the harbor fringes through midday; keep to deeper water. High tide follows at 5:36 PM. At the harbor mouth the current goes slack around 10:43 AM, then floods (setting NNE, up into the harbor) and builds through the afternoon toward 4:25 PM. In the East Passage it's stronger — ebbing seaward until a slack near 11:55 AM, then flooding hard (up to ~0.8 kt) from mid-afternoon. Heads-up: a south wind over an afternoon flood is wind-with-tide early but the real nuisance is the wind-against-wave chop out by the mouth — expect a short, steep 2–3 ft sea out there, so the inner harbor will be much kinder if you go at all.

Good to know

Temps are pleasant for the season (60–65°F), but that's the only easy part. Heavy rain, fog down to a mile or less, and a chance of thunderstorms make this a genuine safety call — poor visibility plus gusts to 20 knots is exactly the combo to sit out. If you do head out, treat it as gusty: life jackets on, reefed and conservative, stay in the inner harbor, and tell someone your plan. Any rumble of thunder or thickening fog means come straight in. Better bet: let this system pass and catch the next clear, breezy day instead.
