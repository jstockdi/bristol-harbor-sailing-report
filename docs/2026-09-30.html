Bristol Harbor · Rhode Island

Wed 30 Sep 2026 · 18:00–20:00

Race window 18:00–20:00

Light SSW (5–7 kt) veering through the window, and a building flood setting NNE up-bay into the harbor — mind the low water at the start.

Wind

Light southerly evening: the fixes average 5–7 kt with gusts to 10–12, and the direction runs SSW (~177°) veering to ~205–210° across the window — a steady clockwise trend of roughly 25–34°. Models are tightly clustered at every fix (spread ≲1 kt inside the harbor), so treat the regime as high-confidence even if the exact numbers are soft.

The gradient favours the seaward end: East Passage / off Hope Island is the strongest fix at 7.4 kt, Off Poppasquash Point 6.6 kt, while the harbor mouth (Hog Island Shoal) and south of Hog sit lighter at ~5.2–5.3 kt and the inner harbor 5.6 kt. Expect more pressure to the S and out the Passage, thinner air inside.

Live obs back a slightly better breeze than the raw model: wind_check has observations running 1.37× the model — lean toward the obs regime. Newport at the Passage mouth is reading ~6 kt SSW (193°) while Conimicut up-bay is a stouter ~10 kt SSE (166°) — a reminder the up-bay air can be both stronger and more southerly than the harbor-mouth number suggests. Bank on 5–7 kt in the harbor with the honest pressure to the S.

Current

Flood, and building. At the harbor mouth (Hog Island NW) slack was at 15:57; the flood then sets NNE up-bay (~011°, into the harbor), growing toward its peak (~0.45 kt) at 21:24 — so the whole 18:00–20:00 window is a rising flood.

The East Passage runs much harder: Dyer Island W went slack at 17:09 and floods NNE (~023°) toward 0.89 kt by 20:42 — better than 0.8 kt of up-bay push through the channel axis during the race. On a flood like this, the fast lane is in the channel when you're heading N/up-bay; if you're working S/seaward, tuck to the shallower shore edges to duck the worst of the NNE set.

Tide

Low water was 16:42 (0.21 ft), just ahead of the start, with the tide rising toward high at 23:27 (3.57 ft). The window sits in the early, still-shallow part of the flood — watch depth at shallow marks and near the harbor edges early on, especially the first hour before the water builds.

Tactical call

Course-agnostic: this is a pressure-plus-current evening in a light, veering SSW. Two levers point the same way — the breeze is stronger to the S/seaward (Poppasquash, and out the Passage) and the flood sets NNE up-bay. When the course lets you, work toward the S/seaward pressure, and use the flood as a free ride when heading N by staying in the channel; when you must go S, hug the shore edges to minimize the foul NNE set. Play the veer: the breeze keeps clocking toward the SW, so the next header/lift sequence trends that direction — stay in phase rather than committing early. Mind the low water at the start for depth near the line.

If the course is set: - N–S windward/leeward — the E–W pressure gradient is modest; the bigger lever is current. Ride the flood N in the channel; on the run/leg S, work the shore edges. Ride the veer for the beat. - J-hook out of Bristol Harbor — you start in near-slack inner harbor (light air), then hook into the building flood at the mouth. The turn flips your current lane — expect the NNE set to help going up-bay and hurt heading seaward. - Around Hog Island — each leg crosses a different quadrant of the flood; the strongest NNE push is through the entrance gut at Hog Island Shoal and in the Passage. Favour the channel on the up-bay leg, the shore edges on the down-bay leg. - Out-and-back (light air) — pressure dominates: get S to the seaward breeze (East Passage 7+ kt) and back; treat the flood as a modest helper home (N) and a modest brake outbound.
