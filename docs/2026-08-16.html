Bristol Harbor · Rhode Island

Sun 16 Aug 2026 · Daysail

Daysail 10:00–18:00

A lovely, easygoing day — light morning that fills to a gentle 8-knot sea breeze by early afternoon; best sailing roughly noon–4pm.

The day

This is a proper mellow-family-daysail kind of day. Sunny skies, comfortable air in the mid-70s, essentially no rain risk, and a soft-to-gentle breeze that never gets pushy. The morning starts light — almost drifter conditions — but the classic afternoon sea breeze should fill in nicely and give you a relaxed 6–8 knots to play with. Nobody's going to get overpowered out there; it's a day for a picnic sail, teaching someone the ropes, or just poking around Hog Island in the sunshine.

Wind

Early on it's genuinely light — the live anemometers at Newport and up at Conimicut are both reading only 4–5 knots out of the N/NNW around 7am, and the models agree it's a slow start. Give it time. As the day warms, the wind clocks around from that light northerly through the E and settles into the familiar S/SSW sea breeze, filling from seaward up the East Passage and over Hog Island. Once it's in, expect a pleasant, steady 7–8 knots with occasional puffs to the mid-teens — nice sailing, never a reef. The models are in tight agreement, so you can trust that picture.

Your best window is roughly noon to 4pm, when the breeze is most established. You'll typically find a touch more air out toward Hog Island and the East Passage than tucked up inside the harbor. And as always, expect a soft spot in the lee of the Poppasquash shore on the west side — if you park there and go quiet, ghost back out toward the middle of the harbor to find pressure again.

Tide & current

High water is at 11:15am (about 4.3 ft), then it drains to a low at 4:47pm — and that afternoon low is quite low (barely a few inches on the gauge). If you're out toward the end of the window, give the shallow edges and marks a wide berth near low tide, especially along the harbor fringes.

Current is gentle inside the harbor and stronger out at the mouth and in the East Passage. It's flooding (setting NNE, up into the harbor) through the morning, goes slack around 12:30pm at the harbor mouth, then turns to a modest ebb (SSW, seaward) in the afternoon. Out in the East Passage the ebb runs about a knot — noticeable if you head out that way after ~2pm. With such light wind there's no meaningful wind-against-tide chop to worry about, so it'll stay comfortable.

Good to know

Beautiful summer conditions: sunny to mostly sunny all day, highs around 77°F, lows around 72°F, and basically zero chance of rain. Bring sunscreen, water, and a hat — with light air and full sun it'll feel warm on the water, so stay hydrated. The breeze is gentle enough that this is an easy, low-stress sail for all levels; still, keep life jackets handy for kids and anyone who wants one, since those mid-teens puffs can arrive without much warning in the afternoon.

A nice rhythm for the day: head out around late morning to catch the building sea breeze, enjoy the sweet spot through the early afternoon, and aim to be back before the tide bottoms out around 4:45pm so you're not threading the shallows on the way in. Have a great sail!
