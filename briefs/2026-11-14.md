## The day
This is a lovely late-season sailing day if you bundle up. A steady northwesterly in the 8–10 kt range gives you a proper, spirited sail without being overpowering — gusts poke into the mid-teens, so you'll be nicely powered up and moving, especially out toward the harbor mouth. The catch is the calendar: it's a cold November afternoon in the low-to-mid 40s, so the air has real bite. If you've got the right layers, go enjoy it — this is a fun, lively daysail with reliable breeze and almost no rain risk.

## Wind
The breeze is up and working already and should hold through the afternoon. Expect NW winds averaging around 8–10 kt, strongest out by Hog Island Shoal and the harbor mouth, and a touch lighter tucked up in the inner harbor and south of Hog Island. Gusts run to the mid-teens, so there's enough to lean on without feeling the need to reef for a standard boat — call it "nicely powered up." The direction slowly backs through the day from the N/NW toward the W, which is normal for a gradient northwesterly, so don't be surprised if your angles shift a bit over a few hours.

The models are in good agreement, and the live anemometers back it up — Conimicut up the bay is reading around 12 kt, Newport at the Passage mouth around 8–9 kt, so the forecast is trustworthy today. Best window is roughly **late morning through mid-afternoon (11:00–15:00)**, when the breeze is most consistent. Watch for a soft, shifty patch under the **western Poppasquash shore** and off its SE side — in a northwesterly that shoreline throws a lee, so expect lighter, puffy air if you sail in close there; stay a bit out into the harbor for steadier wind.

## Tide & current
High water is at **10:46** (about 3.2 ft) and low is at **16:23** (just 0.7 ft), so the afternoon drains down to a shallow low — give the harbor edges and any skinny spots near shore a wide berth as you come back in, especially after 15:00. 

Current is gentle inside the harbor: it floods (up-bay, into the harbor) until slack around **12:09**, then ebbs (down-bay, seaward) through the afternoon. Out toward the **East Passage** the flow is noticeably stronger — ebb builds to nearly a knot after the ~13:45 turn, setting SSW seaward. With the wind out of the NW there's no serious wind-against-tide standoff, so expect only a little added chop near the harbor mouth and in the Passage during the afternoon ebb — nothing a casual sailor needs to worry about, just a slightly livelier ride out there.

## Good to know
It's a chilly one: highs only around 42–45°F under partly sunny to mostly cloudy skies, with essentially no rain (4%) and no thunderstorm concern. The sun's weak this time of year, so dress for the water temperature, not the forecast — warm layers, hat, gloves, and a windproof shell will make all the difference. With gusts into the mid-teens and cold water, **wear your life jacket** and keep a close eye on anyone new to sailing. 

A good plan: head out late morning once the day's warmed as much as it will, enjoy the steady breeze through early-to-mid afternoon, and aim to be back in and tied up by around 15:30–16:00 — ahead of the low tide and before the light and warmth start fading. Have a great sail!
