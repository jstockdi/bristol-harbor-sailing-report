## Wind
A very light November evening. Models and live obs agree closely (fix spreads 0.6–2.3 kt; `wind_check` ratio 0.89, "broadly agree"), so the low-confidence item isn't strength — it's **direction**. Every fix swings from a **NW/NNW start round toward the SE** across the two hours, which is the signature of a dying gradient with no sea breeze to organize it: expect soft, shifty, hole-prone air. Play the puffs and shifts in phase rather than betting a side.

Race-window averages: inner harbor **2.5 kt** (gust 4.7), off Poppasquash **2.2 kt** (gust 5.4), Hog Island mouth **3.2 kt** (gust 9.5), south of Hog **2.8 kt**, East Passage **3.9 kt** (gust 9.5). The **pressure lives seaward** — the Hog mouth and East Passage carry ~1.5 kt more than the inner harbor and the Poppasquash shore is the softest corner. Live anemometers back this up: Newport (Passage mouth) **~2–3 kt NNW**, Conimicut up-bay **~3.7 kt NNW, gusting near 9**. NWS calls NW around 5 kt tonight, becoming NE after midnight.

## Tactical call
**Course-agnostic:** This is a light-air, after-dark night race where **where you point relative to the flood matters more than which shore has breeze**. Two levers:

- **Pressure** sits **seaward** — the Hog Island mouth and East Passage hold the most (and steadiest) air; the inner harbor and the Poppasquash (west) shore are the dead spots. When the course lets you reach toward the S/seaward pressure, take it.
- **Current** floods **NNE up-bay** and strengthens all window. Heading **N (up-bay)** you get a push — ride the deeper channel through the entrance gut and the East Passage axis for max help. Heading **S (seaward)** you're punching the flood — **cheat the shallower shore edges** out of the main NNE set, and don't expect to make a layline against both foul current and a header.

Direction is the wildcard: with the breeze drifting NW→SE, keep the bow on the lifted tack, protect the center, and don't commit to a corner you can't get back from in 2 kt.

**If the course is set:**
- **N–S windward/leeward** — the N beat is current-aided (ride the channel); the S beat is current-foul (hunt the shore edges) *and* lighter. Expect compression and big gains/losses at the leeward end.
- **J-hook out of the harbor** — start in near-slack inner-harbor air, then the hook into the mouth drops you into building flood and better pressure; the favored edge flips once you clear Hog Island Shoal.
- **Around Hog Island** — each leg gets its own call: NNE flood helps the up-bay legs, hurts the seaward legs, and the breeze is best on the island's S/Passage side.
- **Out-and-back (light air)** — pure pressure play: get to the seaward breeze (East Passage) and accept you're fighting the flood on the way out, carried home on the way back.
