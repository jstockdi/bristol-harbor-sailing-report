## The day
This is a proper autumn breeze day — crisp, bright, and powered-up. A west wind of 10–15 knots is blowing across the harbor with gusts into the mid-20s, and the up-bay anemometer at Conimicut is already reading a steady 24 knots. That puts today firmly in the "spirited sail" camp: fun and fast for confident crews who want a lively afternoon, but a touch much for a mellow family drift. Being a westerly, it's blowing off the land, so expect it to be gusty and shifty rather than smooth — plan for the puffs and you'll have a grand time.

## Wind
The breeze is up and steady through the day from the **W/WNW**, averaging around 10–12 knots inside the harbor and a bit lighter (8–9) once you tuck south of Hog Island, but with gusts to 25 across the board — genuinely powered-up, and worth a reef if you've got one and want to keep things relaxed. It's noticeably stronger up-bay toward Conimicut, so the further north you wander the more it'll honk. The models and the live obs are in good agreement, so treat the strength as reliable. Being an offshore westerly, look for a **soft, fluky patch close under the Poppasquash shore (the west side of the harbor)** — handy if you want flatter water and a breather, but the wind there will be patchy and swirly. The breeze should ease back toward evening (into the 5–10 range after dark), so the steadiest, best sailing is **late morning through mid-afternoon**.

## Tide & current
High water was early at 06:44, with a **very low tide at 12:36 (just 0.2 ft)** and the next high around 19:04. That midday low is worth respecting — give the shallow harbor edges and the flats off the Poppasquash shore a wide berth around noon, and mind committee-boat-shallow corners. Current is modest at the harbor mouth (gentle ebb easing to slack around 12:13, then a light flood building late), while out in the East Passage it runs harder — an ebb SSW through the morning to slack near 13:25, then flood after. With the wind out of the W it's mostly across the current rather than against it, so no nasty standing chop to catch you out — just the usual 2–3 ft waves out toward the Passage.

## Good to know
Dress for November: highs only around 53–59°F, so layer up with warm, waterproof gear — you'll feel the wind chill out on the water. The upside is a lovely bright day, going from mostly cloudy to mostly sunny with only a slim (~23%) chance of a passing shower and no thunderstorm worries. Given the gusts into the mid-20s, **life jackets on for everyone** and keep a hand for the boat. A good plan: head out late morning to catch the steady breeze, enjoy the sunshine through early afternoon, and aim to be back at the dock before the light fades and the air turns cold in the evening.
