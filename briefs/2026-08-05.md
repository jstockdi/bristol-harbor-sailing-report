## Wind

Race-window models land at **5.5–7.0 kt averaged, gusts to ~11–12 kt**, and the model spread is tight (0.6–2.1 kt per fix), so treat the *direction* as solid. The **fix gradient is nearly flat**: a touch more pressure off **Poppasquash Point (7.0 kt)** and in the **East Passage (6.7 kt)**, softest right in the **Hog Island Shoal mouth (5.5 kt)** where the island breaks it up. Expect the mouth breeze to back a hair toward the **S (~180°)** as it funnels through.

Confidence caveat on strength: the morning `wind_check` had obs running **weaker than model (0.72×)**, i.e. the model may be over-blowing the numbers. But the live anemometers at 13:54 tell the opposite story for the *afternoon* fill — **Newport 10.3 kt SSW (gusts ~12)** at the Passage mouth and **Conimicut 11.7 kt (peak 16)** up-bay — so the sea breeze had real teeth midday. Bet on it **easing through the evening** toward the forecast 6-ish as the sun drops, but don't be shocked to see 8–10 early in the window.

So while the mouth is already turning to flood, the Passage axis is still draining seaward for most of the race. Mind that split if a leg reaches into the channel.

## Tactical call
Course-agnostic: this is a **pressure-and-current-timing** night, not a big-shift night. The breeze is thin and fairly even across the course, with the small extras sitting **off Poppasquash Point and out toward the East Passage** — when a leg lets you, lean **S/seaward** for the marginal extra knot rather than parking inside the harbor. Early in the window the mouth still carries a light **SSW ebb**; on any seaward leg **stay off the mid-channel SSW set and work the shallower shore edges** — but keep the **19:04 low** in mind, don't cut the shallows too fine. The lever is the **~18:35 mouth slack**: after it, the harbor mouth **floods NNE up-bay**, so a boat heading **N** in the second half wants that new push — while the **East Passage keeps ebbing SSW until ~19:47**, so any leg out in the channel is still fighting seaward flow late.

**If the course is set:**
- **N–S windward/leeward** — cross-course lever is weak (flat E–W pressure); play the **current clock** instead: seaward ebb early, mouth flood on the N legs after ~18:35.
- **J-hook out of the harbor** — near-slack inner-harbor start, then the hook drops you into live water at the mouth; call it by leg — light ebb turning to flood as the race runs.
- **Around Hog Island** — each leg gets its own set: **SSW ebb** on the seaward/mouth side early, **NNE flood** building late, and the strong **East Passage SSW ebb** on any channel-side leg until ~19:47.
- **Out-and-back (light air)** — **pressure dominates**: get out toward the **East Passage / south of Hog** for the breeze, and time the return to ride the **building flood** back up into the harbor.
