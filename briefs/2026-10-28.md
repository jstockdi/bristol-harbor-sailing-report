## Wind

A soft southerly evening race. Models put the window at **5–7 kt** with gusts to
**9–10 kt**, and the fix gradient is the story: pressure sits off **Poppasquash
Point (6.7 kt)** on the west shore and out in the **East Passage (6.2 kt)**, while
**Hog Island Shoal and the water south of the island are the soft spot (4.3–4.7
kt)**. Direction is a steady **S backing toward SSW**, veering ~15–28° across the
window (≈188° → 210°) — plan on a gentle right-hand build as the evening settles.

Confidence is good on direction (model spread ≲2 kt at every fix) and modest on
strength. The live anemometers back a slightly stronger regime than the models:
**Newport reads 7–8 kt from the S** at the Passage mouth, and **Conimicut** up-bay
has gusted to **12.6 kt** (2-hour mean ~5.9). `wind_check` has obs running ~23%
over the model — treat the upper end (7–8 kt, gusts to 10) as realistic. NWS has
SW 5–10 kt. Note this is an after-dark window in late October — bank on fading
light and cool air (low 50s°F).

## Current

Both stations are **flooding and accelerating through the window** — set runs
**NNE up-bay**. At the **harbor mouth (Hog Island NW)** the flood builds from near
the 14:49 ebb-slack toward **max flood 0.47 kt (~011°) at 20:18**, just past the
finish. In the **East Passage (Dyer Island)** it is stronger and peaks mid-race:
**max flood ~0.93 kt (~023°) at 19:36**, not slacking until 22:59.

Because the breeze is from the S and the flood sets N, this is **wind-with-tide**
— expect flat water, no stacked chop. The practical consequence: **anything
heading up-bay (N) gets a push; anything beating S toward Hog Island or into the
Passage fights up to ~0.9 kt of channel-axis flood.**

## Tide

Rising off a low. **Low −0.1 ft at 15:47**, building to **High 3.79 ft at 22:18**,
so the window sits in rising mid-tide with water steadily filling. Early in the
window depth is still modest near the harbor edges — a mild shallow-water caution
for committee-boat placement and any inshore marks, easing as the flood fills in.

## Tactical call

Course-agnostic: the **pressure lives to the W (Poppasquash shore) and seaward
(East Passage)**; the **Hog Island mouth and the water just south of it are the
hole** — avoid parking there. The counterweight is current: the **flood sets NNE
up-bay and strengthens through the race**, strongest in the Passage channel. So on
any leg taking you **S toward Hog Island or the Passage, stay off the mid-channel
flood and work the shallower shore edges**; on any leg **N up-bay, ride the channel
and take the push**. With the breeze veering toward SSW, play the right-hand phase
and don't overstand into the adverse mid-channel set to chase the seaward pressure.

**If the course is set:**
- **N–S windward/leeward** — the E–W gradient is the lever; favor the Poppasquash/
  seaward pressure, but respect the building flood braking the southbound beats.
- **J-hook out of the harbor** — start in the near-slack inner harbor, then hook
  into stronger, building flood at the mouth; the favored edge flips at the turn.
- **Around Hog Island** — give each leg its own call: NNE flood helps the up-bay
  legs and hurts the down-bay ones; the soft air is on the island's S side.
- **Out-and-back (light air)** — pressure dominates; get to the East Passage breeze,
  but mind that the current there is also the strongest adverse flow if you're
  reaching back late.
