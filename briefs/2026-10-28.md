## The day
Honestly, this one's a drifter. Every model and both real anemometers agree the breeze is sitting around 3–4 knots all day, with only the odd little puff to 8–9. That's too light for anything spirited — but if what you want is a mellow, no-pressure afternoon pottering around the harbor with the sails up, maybe a thermos and a friend, it's a lovely calm late-October day to do it. Bring a paddle or be patient, and don't make big plans on crossing the harbor quickly.

## Wind
Early on the air is nearly still and pointing out of the N, then it slowly clocks around through the day toward the S/SSW — classic light-air behavior, just without much muscle behind it. Figure a steady **drifter, 3–4 kt**, nudging a touch stronger (and a hair more reliable) the farther S and seaward you go toward Hog Island and the East Passage mouth. The live anemometers back this up: Newport read about 3 kt WNW just past noon and Conimicut barely a knot, so nobody's getting powered up today. If there's a "best window," it's the **midday-to-mid-afternoon** stretch once the breeze settles into the S and fills what little it's going to fill. Expect the usual soft hole tucked in under the **Poppasquash shore** to the W — if you glide in there and stall, that's why; ease back out toward open water to find what breeze there is.

## Tide & current
High water was at **09:47** (about 4.6 ft) and it drains to a **low around 15:47** that goes slightly negative — so through the afternoon the water's thin. Mind the **shallow edges near the harbor mouth and the shoals off Hog Island and Poppasquash** on that falling tide, and give committee-boat-deep spots and sandbars a wide berth. Current-wise, the harbor itself is sleepy, but it runs harder out at the mouth and especially in the **East Passage**, where the ebb sets SSW (seaward) and can hit well over a knot through the afternoon. In air this light, the current is actually the stronger force out there — if you drift out past Hog Island on the ebb you'll get carried **down-bay to the SSW**, so keep an eye on your drift and don't let the tide take you somewhere the (non-existent) wind can't bring you back from. No wind-against-tide chop to worry about today; seas are a foot or less.

## Good to know
A pleasant, mild fall day — highs around **60°F**, lows in the low 50s, mostly sunny to partly cloudy, and essentially **no rain** (2% chance) and no storm risk. Dress in layers; October water is cold and the breeze off the bay will feel cooler than the air, so bring a jacket for when the sun dips. With winds this light you won't be overpowered, but life jackets on anyway — cold-water season means it's just smart. Head out after late morning once the high tide has peaked and the breeze has clocked S, enjoy the calm, and plan to be back in before the **low around 15:47** leaves you nosing through the skinny water near the harbor edges. Take it as a gift of a quiet day on the water.
