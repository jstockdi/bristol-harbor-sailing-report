## The day
This is a gentle one — a soft, sunny November daysail rather than anything spirited. The models paint a very light picture (low single-digit knots), but the live anemometers out on the bay are already reading a friendlier 5–9 kt from the SSW this afternoon, and NWS has SW 5–10 kt. Translation: a mellow, forgiving day, perfect for a relaxed sail, a new crew, or just enjoying the crisp air and flat water. Don't expect to be hiking out — if anything you'll be hunting for the puffs.

## Wind
Early on the breeze is a whisper out of the N/NW — close to drifter territory inside the harbor. Through late morning into the early afternoon it swings around to the S/SSW and fills to its best, roughly a pleasant 5–9 kt with light gusts, enough to keep things moving nicely without any drama. **Your best window is about 12:30 to 15:30**, when the southerly is most settled. More of the breeze lives out toward the open water — down by Hog Island and into the East Passage — so if it goes glassy up inside, drifting south toward the harbor mouth usually finds a knot or two more. Expect a predictably soft patch tucked under the **Poppasquash shore to the west** on this northerly-then-southerly flow; the air thins out in the lee there, so don't be surprised if you park and have to work your way back into pressure.

## Tide & current
Low water was early, around **08:52 (just 0.7 ft)**, with high tide at **14:49 (about 2.9 ft)** and the next low well after you're home at **20:53**. By the time you launch the water is already rising, so the harbor edges and shallow spots deepen through the afternoon — nice and forgiving, though it's still worth giving the thin water near the shoreline a wide berth early. Current is light and friendly: the harbor mouth floods gently (into the harbor, from the S) until it goes slack around **16:32**, then begins a soft ebb back out. The stronger flow lives out in the East Passage channel, but even there it's modest today. With the southerly breeze and an incoming tide both moving the same general way through the afternoon, there's no meaningful wind-against-tide chop to catch you out — expect a smooth, flat ride.

## Good to know
A beautiful, clear day — **sunny skies, no rain, and highs around 62°F** (dipping toward 50 as evening comes on). Zero thunderstorm risk. The catch is the season: it's November, the water is cold, and once the sun drops the air chills quickly, so **dress warmer than the sunshine suggests** — layers, a windproof shell, and warm gloves will make the light-air sitting-around far more pleasant. In these benign winds you won't need to worry about getting overpowered, but with cold water a cold-weather life jacket is always smart. A good plan: **head out around noon** to catch the best of the southerly, and **aim to be back by 16:00–16:30** so you're off the water comfortably before the breeze fades, the ebb begins, and the late-fall chill sets in.
