## The day
It's a genuinely nice day to get out — bright, dry, and breezy — as long as you bundle up for November. A steady northerly in the 10–15 kt range gives you a spirited but very sailable day, and because the wind is coming off the land to the **N** the harbor water stays relatively flat and forgiving. Think brisk and fun rather than mellow; experienced crews will love it, and even a family sail works fine if you reef down and keep things inside the harbor. The one catch is the air temperature, not the breeze.

## Wind
Expect **N to NNW winds of 10–15 kt** through the day, with gusts into the low 20s — comfortably powered-up, and worth a reef if you want an easygoing afternoon or have lighter crew aboard. The breeze veers just a touch (toward the NNW) as the day goes on. Being a northerly straight off the land, it'll be a little **shifty and puffy**, so expect the odd lull and gust rather than a perfectly steady flow — nothing dramatic, just stay awake on the mainsheet.

There's a real gradient from inside out: up in the **inner harbor** it's a manageable ~11 kt, but down by **Hog Island Shoal** at the mouth and out into the **East Passage** it builds to 14–15 kt with gusts near 23. If you want the mellower ride, stay up inside the harbor; if you want the full-send, head toward the mouth. Soft spots to expect: the **lee tucked south of the land to the N** and along the **Poppasquash shore to the west**, where the wind shadows off — you'll find lighter, puffy patches there.

One honest note on confidence: the live anemometers are reading a bit **lighter than the forecast models** (Newport around 8–11 kt this afternoon, easing some), so the breeze may sit toward the gentler end of that range — though Conimicut up the bay has seen gusts to 25, so respect the puffs. Best window to be out is **late morning through early afternoon**, before the breeze softens later in the day.

## Tide & current
High water is early at **06:51**, then a notably **low low at 12:58 (about −0.3 ft)** — that's a real minus tide, so give the shallow harbor fringes and shoal edges a wide berth around midday; it's easy to find the bottom near the shallows at the top of the low. The next high is at **19:19**, after you're in.

Current is light inside the harbor and stronger out at the mouth and in the **East Passage**. Through the morning it ebbs **SSW (seaward)**, goes slack early afternoon (around 13:00 in the Passage), then the **flood builds back up-bay (NNE) later in the afternoon**. Heads-up: that afternoon flood runs *against* the northerly wind near the harbor mouth and the Passage, which can stand up a short, steeper chop out there — nothing the harbor itself will feel much, but if you poke out around Hog Island later in the day expect it to be bumpier than the water inside.

## Good to know
Clear and **sunny all day**, zero rain, and no thunderstorm worries — a postcard late-November sky. The catch is the cold: highs only around **45–47°F** and dropping into the low 40s, with a northerly wind chill on top. Dress in real layers, bring gloves and a warm hat, and pack a thermos — you'll be glad you did. With gusts into the low 20s this is a **life-jackets-on day**, especially with cold water underfoot; keep a reef handy and don't be shy about sailing a little under-powered for comfort.

Plan to **head out mid-to-late morning** to catch the best of the breeze and the sunshine, and aim to be **back at the dock by mid-afternoon** — the wind eases later, the low midday tide wants respect near the shallows, and the light fades early this time of year. Have a great sail.
