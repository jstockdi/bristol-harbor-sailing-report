## The day
This is a good day to go — just a proper autumn sail rather than a lazy drifter. A steady SW breeze is already filling in across the bay, running a comfortable 10–12 kt in the harbor with gusts toward 20, and more out toward the East Passage. Pair that with crisp, mostly sunny skies and almost no rain risk and you've got a lovely, spirited afternoon. Just dress for November and keep an eye on the gusts — this is a hike-out-and-grin day more than a toes-in-the-water one.

## Wind
The breeze is up and should hold through the afternoon out of the **SW**, nicely consistent in direction all day (the models agree closely, and the live anemometers back them up). Expect a **nice-to-powered-up** 9–11 kt average inside the harbor with puffs into the high teens — enough to get a small boat moving smartly and reward a reef on anything tender. Out past the harbor mouth toward **Hog Island and the East Passage** it's a touch stronger and more open; Newport's gauge is reading mid-teens and Conimicut up the bay has touched close to 20, so the farther south and seaward you wander, the more wind you'll find.

Best window to be out is roughly **late morning through mid-afternoon**, when the sun's up and the breeze is steady. The classic soft spot to expect is tucked in behind **Poppasquash Neck (the west shore)** — the breeze goes light and patchy in its lee, so don't be surprised if you drift there, then find it filling back in as you nose out toward open water.

## Tide & current
High water is right in the middle of your day at **1:45 PM** (about 3.7 ft), so there's plenty of depth for a relaxed sail — no low-water worries during the window (the morning low was back at 7 AM, well before you launch). Current runs gently inside the harbor and picks up through the **gut at Hog Island Shoal** and down the **East Passage**.

One heads-up for comfort: the harbor-mouth current floods (up-bay) through midday, goes slack around **2:40 PM**, then begins to **ebb out to the SSW** in the late afternoon — and the East Passage ebb gets going a bit earlier and runs harder. Since the wind is also from the SW, that late-afternoon ebb sets **against the breeze out near the mouth and in the Passage**, which can stack up a short, bouncy chop. Nothing dramatic, but if you want the smoothest ride, do your open-water sailing before about 4 PM and keep the choppier late-day water in mind.

## Good to know
Cool and bright: highs only around **65°F**, dipping toward the high 50s, with mostly sunny skies and essentially no rain (3% at most). The sun's still pleasant but the **air and water are cold now**, so layer up — a warm mid-layer and a windproof shell, plus something to keep spray off. With gusts pushing 20 kt, **wear your life jacket** and keep it on; a November swim is no fun, and the breeze will find you out past Hog Island.

Simple plan: **head out late morning to early afternoon** to catch the steady breeze and the deep, friendly midday tide, and aim to be **back by late afternoon** before the ebb-against-wind chop builds and the light starts to fade. Enjoy it — crisp, sunny, and sailing — that's a fine way to spend a fall day on Bristol Harbor.
