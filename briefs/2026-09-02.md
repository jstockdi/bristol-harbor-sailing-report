## Tactical call

Course-agnostic: this is a **light-air, pressure-first night**. The breeze lives **inside the harbor and up-bay (Poppasquash/Bristol waterfront side)** and thins toward Hog Island and the East Passage, so **don't chase the seaward legs expecting more wind — you'll find less**. Current is a secondary lever tonight: near-slack early, then a **weak-to-moderate NNE flood** that conveniently pushes the same way the pressure wants you (up-bay, N). Where the course sends you **N/up-bay, ride the building flood in the channel**; where it sends you **S/seaward, you're fighting both the thinning breeze and (after 18:24) a foul flood — hunt the shallower shore edges to duck the strongest set** through the entrance gut.

**If the course is set:**
- **N–S windward/leeward** — the up-harbor (N) end holds the breeze; play the E–W pressure gradient toward the Poppasquash/harbor side, and use the NNE flood as a bonus up the beat, a brake down the run.

- **Around Hog Island** — legs heading up-bay ride the flood; the S/seaward legs are the light, current-against pain point — take transoms cautiously and work the island tips and shore edges out of the gut.
- **Out-and-back (light air)** — pressure dominates and it's stacked **up-harbor**, so the seaward run is the risk; get back into the harbor breeze quickly and use the NNE flood on the return.
