## The day
This is a **spirited one** — a crisp October northeaster with a real edge to it. The breeze is honestly up: steady mid-teens to high-teens with gusts into the high 20s, and the NWS marine forecast is calling NE 20–25 kt gusting 30 out in the Bay. If you love being powered-up and sailing on the rail, it's a great day to go stretch the boat's legs. If you were hoping for a lazy, flat-water family cruise, this one leans toward **marginal** — fun for experienced hands who'll tuck in a reef, a bit much for new sailors or little ones. Dress warm either way; it's a cool, grey fall afternoon.

## Wind
Expect the breeze **from the north-northeast all day** (roughly 015–035°), and it's in well already — the live anemometers are reading honest: Newport at the Passage mouth is averaging about **16 kt with peaks near 19**, and Conimicut up the Bay is even stronger at **17–18 kt average, peaking into the low 20s**. Forecast and reality agree closely, so take the strength at face value: this is a **reef-it-if-you-have-one**, two-hands-on-the-sheet kind of day, not a drifter. It'll feel most honest out toward **Hog Island and the East Passage**, where it's a knot or two stronger and cleaner; a touch softer up inside the harbor. Because the wind is coming down off the Bristol and **Poppasquash** land to the north, look for it to be **puffy and shifty** — gusts arriving in waves rather than a smooth river of air. Tuck in close under the **Poppasquash shore** (to its east/southeast) if you want a calmer patch to catch your breath or shake out a reef; the land there knocks the breeze down and softens the chop. The strongest, steadiest window is **early-to-mid afternoon**, which also happens to be when the water lies down a bit (see below).

## Tide & current
**Low water is right at 10:31 this morning (just 0.7 ft)** and **high is at 16:42** — so the harbor is at its skinniest early. If you're heading out near launch time, **give the shallow harbor edges and any unmarked shoals a wide berth** until the water builds back through the afternoon. For current: the harbor mouth off Hog Island and the East Passage are near **slack late morning (~10–11:15)**, then **flood builds through the afternoon, peaking around 15:00–15:45** before easing to slack around 18:00. The good news — that afternoon flood runs **up-bay to the NNE, the same direction the wind is blowing**, so wind and current are working *together* and the chop stays more organized. Earlier in the morning the last of the ebb runs **against** the breeze, which can kick up short, steep little waves near the mouth — one more reason the afternoon is the sweeter window. Waves around 3 ft are forecast out in the Passage, so expect a livelier ride once you clear Hog Island.

## Good to know
It's a **cool, cloudy fall day** — highs only around **51–54°F**, mostly cloudy with a few breaks of sun, and essentially **no rain** (11% at most) and no thunderstorm risk. Dress in real layers, bring a windproof shell and gloves; with the wind up and the air this cool, you'll feel the chill fast once you're wet or sitting still. **This is a life-jacket-on day** — the gusts touch the high 20s, so wear it, clip in if you've got jacklines, and reef early rather than wishing you had. A good plan: **head out early-to-mid afternoon** when the water's come back up and the flood is smoothing things out, and aim to **be back by around 16:30–17:00** before the light fades and the evening chill sets in. Go sail it if you're up for a brisk one — just rig for the breeze, not for a drifter.
