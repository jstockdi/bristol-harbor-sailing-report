## The day
It's a pleasant, low-key November daysail — nothing dramatic. A light-to-moderate northwesterly, roughly 5–8 knots with the odd gust to 11–12, means easy cruising with maybe a lively puff now and then rather than any white-knuckle stuff. Being an offshore (northerly) breeze it'll be a touch shifty and puffy, so expect the wind to come and go in patches. Two things to plan around: a very low tide right at midday, and patchy fog that could cut visibility, so pick your moment and keep a casual eye out.

## Wind
The breeze is from the **NW–NNW** (roughly 330–345°), fairly steady in direction all day. The forecast models are tightly clustered around 5–7 kt and the live anemometers back that up — Newport at the Passage mouth has been averaging ~9 kt and Conimicut up-bay ~10 kt over the last couple of hours, so if anything you may find it a shade friendlier than the 5–7 kt numbers, especially once you get out toward **Hog Island** and the **East Passage**, which tend to hold a knot or two more pressure. That's comfortable, nicely-powered sailing for most boats — no reef needed, but enough to keep things fun.

Because it's blowing off the land, look for **soft spots in the lee of the Poppasquash shore** (the west side of the harbor) — if you park in there the breeze will go light and fluky, so stay a little further out toward open water for cleaner air. Best window is late morning through mid-afternoon; the breeze is forecast to build this evening (15–20+ kt after dark, outside your sailing window), so there's no need to be out late.

## Tide & current
High water is early at **06:01**, then it drains to a **very low tide around noon (12:03, only ~0.2 ft)** before filling again to a high at **18:21**. That midday low is the thing to respect — give the shallow harbor edges, the flats off Poppasquash, and any skinny spots near the shore a wide berth around late morning to early afternoon, and don't cut corners near the moorings.

Current is gentle inside the harbor. Out at the mouth it's slack around **11:30** and then floods (gently, up into the harbor) through the afternoon; in the **East Passage** it's slack about **12:42** before a modest flood builds later. With the NW wind blowing roughly against that incoming flood out in the Passage you might feel a little short, lumpy chop out past Hog Island in the afternoon — nothing alarming in this breeze, just a heads-up so it doesn't surprise you.

## Good to know
It's a cool autumn day — air temps **54–64°F**, a mix of mostly cloudy and partly sunny skies, and only a slight (≤~17%) chance of a passing shower. The one to watch is **patchy fog**: visibility could drop to a mile or less at times, so dress in layers, bring a warm/waterproof top, and if the fog rolls in thick, stay close to home and keep track of your bearings. Remember the water's chilly this time of year, so **wear your life jacket** — easy to do and worth it with cold water and the occasional gust about.

A nice plan: head out mid-to-late morning once things warm up a touch, enjoy the afternoon breeze, and aim to be back by mid-to-late afternoon before the wind starts building toward evening and the light starts going. Have a great sail!
