## The day
This is a lovely low-key autumn day to go for a sail — bright sun, clear skies, and a soft northeasterly that should give you just enough to move without ever being a handful. Think easy cruising rather than a thrill ride: the breeze is on the light side, so it's a mellow family daysail, with maybe a drifter moment or two if it goes soft. The one catch is the chill — it'll be a brisk fall day, so the sailing is gentle but you'll want to dress for it.

## Wind
Expect a gentle **northeasterly** all day, gradually swinging around toward the **east** as the afternoon goes on. The forecast models were quite light (3–4 kt), but the real anemometers at Newport and Conimicut are already reading a steadier **6–10 kt from the NNE** this morning, and NWS calls NE 5 to 10 kt — so the on-the-water reality looks a touch friendlier than the raw model numbers. Call it a pleasant, easy breeze: enough for relaxed sailing, occasionally dipping to drifter territory in the lulls.

Your **best window is roughly late morning into early afternoon**, while the breeze is holding its best. Out toward the **harbor mouth by Hog Island and into the East Passage** you'll find the most consistent air — a knot or so more than up inside the harbor. Expect **soft, shifty patches tucked under the land**, especially along the **Poppasquash shore to the west** where the northerly component gets blanketed. If you go quiet, just ghost out toward more open water to the **south** and you'll find it again.

## Tide & current
**High water is at 2:03 PM** (about 2.9 ft), right in the middle of your day — so plenty of depth everywhere, no shallow worries to speak of. Lows are well outside your window (early morning and after 8 PM).

Current is light and nothing to fret about. At the harbor mouth the flood (gently setting **up-bay, NNE into the harbor**) runs through the early afternoon, easing to slack around **3:30 PM**, then a mild ebb (setting **SSW, seaward**) builds after. Out in the **East Passage** the flow is a bit stronger — slack near **3:00 PM**, then an afternoon ebb. One small heads-up: after about **5 PM** the NE breeze blowing against that seaward-running ebb can kick up a little short chop near the harbor mouth and in the Passage. It's minor in this light air, but it's a reason to point homeward before then rather than linger out in the entrance.

## Good to know
Dress warmly — it's a classic crisp fall day, highs only around **55°F** and dipping toward the upper 40s, so bring layers, a windbreaker, and maybe gloves. The payoff is a gorgeous forecast: **sunny and clear, no rain, no thunderstorm risk at all.** Sun's lower this time of year, but still bring sunglasses and a hat.

A simple plan: **head out around late morning**, enjoy the best of the breeze and the midday high tide, and **aim to be back by late afternoon** (before 5 PM) to skip any wind-against-tide slop at the harbor mouth and to beat the autumn chill as the sun gets low. Winds stay gentle enough that life jackets are just good standard practice here — nothing gusty to worry about today. Have a great sail!
