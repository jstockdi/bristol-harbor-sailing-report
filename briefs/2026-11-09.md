## The day
It's a good sailing day if you dress for fall and like a little zip in the rig. A steady WNW breeze in the 10–13 kt range with gusts into the low-20s makes for a **spirited daysail** — plenty of power without being overwhelming for an experienced crew. Newer or lighter sailors might find the gusts a handful, so think about a smaller headsail or a tuck in the main and keep an easy escape back to the harbor. The air is cool (low-to-mid 50s) under mostly sunny skies, so this is a brisk, bracing kind of day rather than a lazy drifter.

## Wind
The breeze is up from the start and holds through the afternoon — no waiting around for it to fill. It's blowing **from the WNW (roughly 270–290°)**, the classic shifty, puffy offshore-land wind, so expect it to come in pulses and swing a bit; stay alert on the sheets and you'll enjoy it. Out in the open water off Hog Island's mouth and down the East Passage you'll feel the most pressure (12 kt-ish, powered-up), while the live anemometers up the bay are already gusting into the high-teens to low-20s — so it's **reef-it territory in the puffs** if you're sailing short-handed. The one reliably soft, sheltered spot is **tucked in behind Poppasquash Neck on its east/southeast side**, where the land blocks the WNW breeze — a handy place to duck in for a calmer moment or to shake out and re-set. Forecast and the real wind readings agree closely today, so you can trust this picture.

## Tide & current
High water was early (**07:24**), with **low water at 13:10 dropping to just about 0.2 ft** — that's a very low tide, so **give the shallow edges and mud flats near the harbor fringes a wide berth** through the early afternoon. Current in the harbor itself is gentle; the real flow runs through the gut by **Hog Island Shoal** and out in the **East Passage**, where it ebbs seaward (toward the SSW) until about **slack near 13:00–14:00**, then turns to flood back up-bay. With a WNW breeze crossing that moving water you may notice some **short, lumpy chop out in the open Passage**, especially over the ebb mid-morning to midday — nothing dramatic, but a casual sailor should expect a bit of slap and spray out there versus the smoother water inside the harbor.

## Good to know
Dress warm — it's a 50–56°F day with a cool breeze that'll feel cooler still with the wind-chill out on the water, so layers, a hat, and something to block spray will keep you happy. Skies are **mostly sunny with just a few passing rain showers possible** (under a 30% chance), so you might catch a quick sprinkle but nothing stormy — no thunder in the picture. Because it'll be **gusty into the low-20s, this is a life-jacket-on, hook-in kind of day**, and a good one to sail with a buddy rather than solo. Head out mid-morning to make the most of the steady breeze and the smoother earlier water, and aim to be back by **mid-to-late afternoon** — the light fades early this time of year and the breeze stays up, so don't linger past the golden hour.
