## The day
This is a lovely late-season day to get out — call it spirited rather than mellow. A steady SW breeze in the 10-knot range with gusts toward 20 means lively, powered-up sailing under sun and high cloud, with warm-for-November air in the mid-60s. It's a touch much for a brand-new sailor's first solo, but for anyone comfortable on a reach it'll be a treat. Keep a reef in your back pocket and you'll have a grand time.

## Wind
The breeze is already up and blowing from the SW, and it holds that direction and strength all day — no need to wait for a sea breeze to build this time of year. Figure a solid, consistent SW flow: nicely powered-up in the lulls and a hands-full 18–20 in the gusts. The live anemometers are running a bit stronger than the forecast models (Newport's reading 14 gusting 17, Conimicut up-bay nudging 18), so trust the brisk end of the range — dress and rig for the puffs, not the averages. The best stretch is late morning straight through mid-afternoon; it eases a little toward dusk but stays sailable. Expect a softer, flukier patch tucked in behind **Poppasquash Neck** on the west shore, where the point throws a lee — nice if you want a breather, but the breeze will fan back in as you work out toward open water. More consistent pressure sits out toward **Hog Island** and the mouth.

## Tide & current
Low water is right at 10:02 (only about half a foot) and high is 15:50. With that early low, give the shallow harbor edges and shoals a wide berth first thing — stay to deeper water until the tide starts flooding back in through midday. Current inside the harbor is gentle all day (near slack much of the morning, a weak flood setting up-bay into the harbor through the afternoon). Out at the **harbor mouth by Hog Island** and in the **East Passage** it runs a bit harder — the afternoon flood there sets NNE up-bay, which runs *with* today's SW wind, so you'll find things pleasantly flat rather than choppy. No nasty wind-against-tide slop to catch you out.

## Good to know
A beautiful autumn day: sunshine giving way to some high cloud, highs 61–68°F, and essentially no rain (3% chance) — the wet weather holds off until Thursday night. That said, the water's getting cold for November and the gusts reach 20 knots, so this is a **life-jacket-on day** for everyone aboard, with a warm layer and a windbreaker. Head out late morning once the tide's begun to fill back in, enjoy the prime early-afternoon breeze, and aim to be back at the dock by late afternoon as the light and the wind both start to fade. Have fun out there!
