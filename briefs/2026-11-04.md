## Tactical call
**Course-agnostic:** The levers point the same way — **pressure lives to the S/seaward (Poppasquash Point, Hog Island south, East Passage), and the ebb sets SSW down the Passage.** With the breeze from the SW and the ebb running SSW, the two are roughly aligned on the channel axis. On any **beat, the extra breeze off Poppasquash Point and seaward of Hog is worth chasing** — but temper it against the current: when a leg points you **up-bay (N, toward/into the harbor), you're punching the ebb**, so hunt the shallower shore edges where the SSW set is weakest and stay out of the mid-channel conveyor; when a leg takes you **seaward (S), ride the channel ebb** for the free push. The inner harbor is light and near-slack — don't expect either pressure or current to pay in there.

**If the course is set:**
- **N–S windward/leeward:** The E–W pressure band (lighter harbor/mouth, more off Poppasquash & seaward) is the cross-course lever on the beat; on the run S, the channel ebb adds to VMG — take it.
- **J-hook out of the harbor:** Start in the quiet near-slack inner harbor, then the hook into the mouth/Passage drops you straight into the building SSW ebb and more breeze — the favored edge flips seaward the moment you clear Hog Island Shoal.
- **Around Hog Island:** Give each leg its own call — the SSW ebb helps on the seaward/southbound leg and hurts on the up-bay/northbound leg, where you'll want the shore edges off the island tips rather than the gut.
- **Out-and-back (light air):** Not really a light-air night with obs at 10–14 kt, but if it softens, get to the seaward breeze first; the ebb is a bonus going out and a tax coming home.
