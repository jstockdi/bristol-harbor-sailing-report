## The day
This is a really pleasant day to be on the water — a proper "just go sailing" kind of afternoon. A steady southerly of around 10–11 knots is set up all day, which is enough to keep things lively and fun without being a handful. Skies lean mostly cloudy with sunny breaks and just a small chance of a passing shower, and it's a comfortable mid-70s. Call it a good family daysail with a bit of spice: relaxed enough for a mellow cruise, but with puffs into the high teens that'll put a smile on anyone who likes to heel over a little.

## Wind
The breeze is already filling this morning from the **south** and holds nicely through the whole daysailing window — no waiting around for it to build. Expect a solid **10–11 knots** across the harbor and out toward Hog Island, gusting into the mid-to-high teens (and up around **20 kt** out in the East Passage). In plain terms: comfortably powered-up, occasionally enough to want a hand ready on the mainsheet in the bigger puffs, but nowhere near reef territory for most boats.

The models are in tight agreement (barely a knot of spread), and the live anemometers at Newport and Conimicut are reading a touch under forecast this morning (7–9 kt) as it fills in — so don't be surprised if the first hour or two feels a shade lighter before it settles into that steady 10–11. There's a bit more pressure the farther **south** you go, toward Hog Island and out into the East Passage, and slightly less tucked up inside the harbor. The one soft spot to expect is close under the **Poppasquash shore to the west** — the land takes a bite out of a southerly there, so if you find yourself parked, ease out toward open water and the breeze fills back in. Best window is honestly the whole afternoon, but **late morning through mid-afternoon** is the sweet spot.

## Tide & current
High water was around **7:47 AM**, dropping to a notably **low tide at 1:05 PM (just 0.4 ft)**, then filling back to a high at **8:06 PM**. That midday low is on the shallow side, so give the harbor edges, sandbars, and any thin spots near the shore a wide berth around lunchtime — keep to deeper water in the middle of the day.

Current is gentle inside the harbor and picks up out at the **harbor mouth off Hog Island** and in the **East Passage**. Through the morning it's ebbing (running seaward to the **SSW**), goes slack early afternoon (around 1–2:30 PM near the mouth and Passage), then turns to flood (up-bay to the **NNE**) later in the day. Since the wind is out of the south, the morning ebb runs roughly *with* the breeze — nice and smooth. Heads-up for the late afternoon: once the flood kicks in against that southerly, you may notice a bit of short, steep **wind-against-tide chop** out in the East Passage (where the current runs hardest, around a knot). Nothing alarming, just a slightly bumpier ride if you head out that way toward evening.

## Good to know
Comfortable all day — highs around **74–75°F**, mostly cloudy with some sun and only about an **18% chance of a shower or thunderstorm**. Keep half an eye on the sky, especially over the land to the west; if you see a cell building, head in early, as pop-up thunderstorms can bring quick gusts. Even with clouds, wear sunscreen and bring water. With gusts pushing into the high teens (and near 20 out in the Passage), it's a sensible **life-jackets-on** kind of day, especially for kids and less experienced crew. A good plan: head out **late morning** to catch the settled breeze, enjoy the afternoon, and aim to be **back before evening** to skip the late-day chop out in the Passage and stay ahead of any stray storm.
