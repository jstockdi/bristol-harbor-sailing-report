## The day
It's a crisp, sunny October day with real wind behind it — a spirited sail rather than a lazy one. A steady northeasterly in the low-to-mid teens, gusting into the 20s (and the open water near the harbor mouth touching 30), means this is a day for sailors who like a little giddy-up and don't mind bundling up. Families with small kids or anyone wanting a gentle drift might want to sit this one out or stay well up inside the harbor; everyone else is in for a clear-skied, powered-up afternoon. Reef early, keep it fun, and you'll have a great time.

## Wind
The breeze is up from the start and stays up all day — no waiting around for it to fill. Expect NE wind averaging around 13–15 kt through the harbor, a touch stronger (15–16 kt) out toward Hog Island and the East Passage, with gusts well into the 20s. That's solidly powered-up for most boats and reef-it territory for smaller ones, especially out in the open. The live anemometers back this up: Newport at the Passage mouth is reading about 11–12 kt, while Conimicut up the bay is already blowing 17 gusting 24 — so the farther up-bay and out of the harbor you go, the more punch you'll feel. The models all agree closely, so treat this forecast as dependable.

Because it's a northeasterly coming off the land, expect it to be a bit shifty and puffy rather than smooth. The calmest, most forgiving water will be tucked in the lee of the Bristol and Poppasquash shores on the north and east sides — handy soft patches if you want to catch your breath or shake out a reef. Your best window for a comfortable sail is the **early-to-mid afternoon**, roughly 13:00–16:00, when the current turns to run with the wind and the chop lays down a little.

## Tide & current
Low water is at **09:30** (just under a foot) and high at **15:47** (about 3 ft), with the harbor filling all morning and into the afternoon. Around that morning low, give the shallow edges near the harbor shoreline and the flats off Poppasquash a wide berth — there's not much water over them.

Current runs hardest through the entrance gut by Hog Island Shoal and out in the East Passage channel; it's close to slack inside the harbor itself. Here's the thing to watch: in the **morning the tide is ebbing seaward (to the SSW) against the NE wind**, so you'll find steeper, bouncier chop out near the mouth and in the Passage until the current goes slack around 10:00–10:30. After that it flips to a gentler flood running up-bay through the afternoon — wind and current more aligned, friendlier water. If you want the smoother ride, aim for the afternoon.

## Good to know
Gorgeous autumn sky — mostly sunny all day, no rain in the picture (0% chance). It'll be cool, though: highs only around 58–60°F and dropping toward the low 50s, and that NE breeze over the water will feel chillier still. Dress in real layers, bring a windbreaker or foulies, and don't underestimate how cold hands get out there in October. With gusts regularly into the 20s and touching 30 near the open water, this is a **life-jackets-on, hook-in day** — keep the crew clipped and ready.

Head out late morning once that morning ebb-chop eases, enjoy the brightest, best-mannered breeze through the early afternoon, and plan to be back at the dock by late afternoon before the light fades and the wind builds further into the evening. Have a blast — just respect the gusts.
