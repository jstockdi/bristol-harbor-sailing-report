## The day
This is a proper good day to go sailing. A mellow-to-just-right southwesterly settles in over the harbor, running a steady 8 knots or so with occasional puffs into the low teens — enough to fill the sails and make a nice heel without ever feeling like a handful. Warm, mostly sunny, and hardly any rain risk. It's a relaxed family daysail with just enough breeze to keep it fun. Go enjoy it.

## Wind
The morning starts soft — the live anemometers off Newport and up at Conimicut were only reading about 5 knots at breakfast, so if you're on the water early expect a gentle drifter and be patient. The classic SW sea breeze builds through late morning and settles into a pleasant 8-knot working breeze from the southwest (around 235–240°), gusting to 14–15 in the puffs. The models are in tight agreement on this, so you can trust it. **The sweet spot is roughly noon through late afternoon** — that's when you'll get the most consistent pressure.

You'll find a little more air out toward Hog Island and the harbor mouth, and a soft, patchy spot tucked in under the **Poppasquash shore to the west** — if you go quiet there, that's why; nudge back out toward the middle of the harbor and the breeze returns. Nothing here needs a reef, but keep a hand on the mainsheet for the gusts.

## Tide & current
Low water comes early, around **9:17 AM, and it's a big low (just 0.3 ft)** — so early birds should give the shallow edges and mudflats near the harbor fringes a wide berth until the water starts coming back. From there the tide fills nicely all afternoon to a **high around 4:33 PM (4.0 ft)** — plenty of water for wherever you want to poke around.

Current is gentle inside the harbor and never a problem for a daysail. Through the afternoon it's a light flood setting up-bay (to the NNE, into the harbor), which runs roughly with the SW wind, so you won't see any nasty chop. It's stronger out in the East Passage channel if you venture that way, but still modest. The wind-against-tide ebb doesn't kick in until after 7 PM, well past when you'd be heading home.

## Good to know
Warm summer day — highs 78–83°F, mostly sunny early trending to some afternoon clouds, and only about a 10% rain chance, so no real thunderstorm worry. Bring water, sunscreen, and a hat; the sun on the water adds up. It never gets truly gusty, but with puffs to the mid-teens it's a sensible day to have life jackets on the little ones and within easy reach for everyone. **Head out around late morning to catch the breeze filling in, aim for the noon-to-4 window for the best sailing, and be back before the early-evening tide turns.** Have a great sail.
