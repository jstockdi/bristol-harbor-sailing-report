## The day
This is an easy, low-stress family daysail — nothing to be intimidated by. The morning starts soft and driftery, but a proper southerly sea breeze builds through the afternoon, so if you time it right you'll get a lovely, relaxed sail without ever feeling over-powered. Think mellow rather than spirited: enough breeze to move nicely, gentle enough for kids, guests, or a leisurely cruise around Hog Island. The one thing to keep half an eye on is a small chance of an isolated afternoon shower.

## Wind
Early on it's light and shifty — genuine drifter territory in the harbor (single digits, direction wandering out of the west). As the day warms, the classic Bristol sea breeze fills in from the **S/SSW**, coming up the East Passage past Hog Island and steadying things out. Forecast numbers are modest (averages in the 4–5 kt range with gusts to 9–11), but the live buoys are already telling a friendlier story: Newport at the Passage mouth is reading 5–7 kt and Conimicut up-bay is a solid 10–11 kt from the south — so out on the open water south of Hog Island and toward the East Passage you'll likely find the nicest, most reliable pressure. Expect a **soft patch tucked under the Poppasquash shore** (the western side) when the breeze is westerly early — if you park there, just be patient and work back out toward open water. **Best window: early-to-mid afternoon onward**, once the southerly has properly settled in. Nice, comfortable sailing — no reefing required.

## Tide & current
Highs are at **08:30** and again at **20:46**; the low is at **14:12 — and it's a very low one (basically zero on the gauge)**. That means midday through mid-afternoon is the time to give the shallow harbor edges and any mudflats a wide berth — stay to charted water near the shorelines and around the harbor mouth. Current is gentle inside the harbor (near-slack around **10:00** and again at **14:00** at the mouth), with a modest **ebb setting seaward (SSW)** through the middle of the day. Out in the **East Passage** the flow is stronger (over a knot on the ebb around late morning), and since the afternoon southerly blows *against* that ebb, you may feel a little short, bouncy chop out there around midday — nothing dangerous, just a heads-up so it doesn't surprise you. It smooths out as the ebb eases toward 15:00.

## Good to know
Comfortable late-summer conditions: highs around **78°F**, dipping to the upper 60s, with a mix of sun and clouds. There's an **18% chance of an isolated shower or thunderstorm** — low, but worth a glance at the sky, and if you see a dark cell building, head in early rather than wait it out. Bring water, sunscreen, and a light layer for when the sea breeze cools things off. A good plan: **head out around midday to early afternoon** to catch the filling breeze, and aim to **be back by early evening** as the wind softens and the light fades. It's a gentle day, but keep life jackets handy for the kids and anyone new to sailing — the afternoon gusts are friendly, not fierce. Have a great sail!
