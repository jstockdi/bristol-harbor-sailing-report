## The day
This is a genuinely good day to be on the water — a bright early-autumn afternoon with a steady, honest SSW breeze. It's more spirited than mellow: you'll be powered up and sailing properly rather than drifting, with enough in the gusts to keep you honest. A fit, attentive crew will love it; if you've got little ones or a very casual boat aboard, you'll want a reef handy and an eye on the puffs. Nothing extreme — just a solid, satisfying sail.

## Wind
The breeze is already in and working: the real anemometers down at the East Passage mouth and up at Conimicut are reading **13–15 kt from the SSW** around midday, and the forecast backs that up through the afternoon. Expect a sustained **SSW (around 205–220°) at roughly 10–14 kt**, with **gusts into the low 20s** — firmly in powered-up territory, occasionally wanting a touch of easing in the stronger puffs. Models and the live obs agree nicely, so you can trust this one.

The breeze is a classic southerly funneling up from the East Passage, so you'll find it a hair stronger and steadier the farther **south toward Hog Island and the Passage mouth** you go. The reliable soft spot is tucked in the lee of **Poppasquash Neck (the west shore)** — if you duck in there for a calmer patch you'll lose some pressure, and the wind will swing around as you slide back out into the open harbor. Good to know if you want a breather, or want to avoid the lull.

Best window to be out is roughly **noon to 4 PM**, when the breeze is most established.

## Tide & current
High water is at **12:52 PM (about 4 ft)**, and it drops to a **low around 6:46 PM (0.7 ft)** — so the back half of your afternoon is a falling, draining tide. Keep clear of the **shallow harbor edges and any skinny water near the shoreline** as the afternoon wears on, especially if you're out past 5.

For current: at the harbor mouth off Hog Island the tide is flooding in (setting up-bay, to the NNE) through about **1:40 PM**, then turns to ebb (setting seaward, SSW) in the mid-afternoon. In the morning-to-midday stretch, that incoming flood runs *against* the SSW breeze — so expect some **short, lumpy chop at the harbor mouth and out in the East Passage**; nothing alarming, just a bit of bounce so you're not caught off guard. Once the tide turns to ebb after ~1:40, current and wind line up the same way and things smooth out. Note the East Passage current runs stronger (around a knot) than inside the harbor, so the open water will always feel livelier than the sheltered inner harbor.

## Good to know
A lovely fall day: highs around **74°F**, dipping toward the low 60s, with **sunshine giving way to more clouds later** and only a **slim (~24%) chance of a passing shower** — no thunderstorm concern. It'll feel cooler out on the water with that breeze, so **bring a layer or a spray top** — this is not a shorts-and-t-shirt-only kind of sail once the sun's off you.

With gusts into the low 20s, this is a **life-jackets-on day** for everyone, and a good day to have the reef lines rigged before you leave the mooring. Head out around **late morning to midday** to catch the best of the breeze, and plan to be **back by around 5 PM** — that keeps you off the draining low-tide shallows and in with plenty of daylight. Have a great sail!
