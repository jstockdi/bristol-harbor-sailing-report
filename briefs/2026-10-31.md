## The day
It's a bright, sailable Halloween — sunny skies, dry air, and a steady northerly that's got some spirit to it. Call it a lively-but-manageable day: a comfortable 8–10 kt through most of the harbor with gusts into the high teens and low 20s, so it leans toward the "fun and powered-up" end rather than a mellow drifter. If you like a bit of a sleigh ride you'll love it; if you're out with little ones or newer crew, keep things reefed-down and you'll still have a great afternoon. Cool temps mean layers are the price of admission, but the payoff is clean, sparkling late-October sailing.

## Wind
The breeze is up and blowing from the **N–NW** right from the start of the window and holds through the afternoon before the NWS has it easing toward evening (NW dropping to 5–10 kt). Expect a solid working breeze of **8–10 kt in the harbor**, a touch more out toward the **harbor mouth and the East Passage (9–10 kt)**, with gusts to **20 kt-plus** — so plenty to keep you moving and the occasional puff to mind. That's firmly in "nicely powered-up" territory, trending toward "think about a reef" in the gusts, especially the farther out you go.

Two things worth knowing: the up-bay anemometer at **Conimicut Light** is already reading a stiff **17 kt gusting 22** from the NW, while **Newport** at the Passage mouth is a gentler **10–12 kt** — so the north end of the bay is breezier than the mouth, which is typical of a northerly funneling down. And with wind out of the N/NW, look for a **soft, puffy patch tucking in behind Poppasquash Point on its E/SE side** — the shore throws a lee there, so expect lighter, shiftier air close under that western shore and steadier breeze out in the open harbor.

Best window to be out is **late morning through mid-afternoon**, while the breeze is full and the sun is high; it softens toward evening.

## Tide & current
High water is right around **12:40 PM (4.0 ft)**, and the tide drains all afternoon toward a **low near 7:05 PM (0.5 ft)** — so the water's generous at midday but getting thin by the end of the window. Give the **shallow harbor edges and shoal bits near the shoreline a wide berth after about 4 PM** as it drops out.

On the current: the strongest flow is out at the **harbor mouth around Hog Island** and in the **East Passage**, near-slack inside the harbor itself. In the **morning the flood is setting up-bay (NNE) against the northerly wind** — that wind-against-tide combo can stand up a little **short, bumpy chop at the mouth and in the Passage**, so don't be surprised by it. Things ease through the midday slack (mouth around **1:20 PM**, Passage around **1 PM**), and in the **afternoon the ebb turns to run seaward (SSW) with the wind**, which lays the chop down and makes for a smoother ride. The Passage ebb runs a real **1 kt**, so it'll carry you along out there.

## Good to know
It's a cool one — **48–54°F**, sunny and clear with **no rain in the forecast** — so dress for the water, not the sun: layers, a windbreaker, and gloves won't go amiss in that NW breeze. The sun's lower and the days are short now, so plan to be **off the water comfortably before it starts to fade** (late afternoon). With gusts into the low-to-mid 20s, this is a **life-jackets-on kind of day** — nothing alarming, just a breeze with some punch, especially out toward the mouth and up-bay where it's stiffest. Head out **late morning** for the best of it, keep an eye on your sail trim in the puffs, and aim to be back in before the tide gets thin and the breeze softens toward evening. Happy Halloween sailing!
