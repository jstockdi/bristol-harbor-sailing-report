## The day
This is a easygoing, low-key kind of day — a classic mellow family daysail rather than anything spirited. Winds are light throughout, in the 4–6 knot range with only occasional puffs into the low teens, so you'll be ghosting along on a light air boat rather than heeling hard. The morning starts soft and northerly, then a light southerly sea breeze fills in from the East Passage after midday. If you love a calm, relaxed sail with plenty of time to soak up the harbor, this is your day. If you're after a rail-down thrill ride, this one's too gentle.

## Wind
Early on it's light and out of the **north** — a drifter, honestly, with the live anemometers at Newport and Conimicut both reading only 2–3 knots first thing. Through late morning the wind fades and swings around as the summer sea breeze tries to organize itself, and by early-to-mid afternoon it should settle into a light **southerly** coming up the East Passage — think 5–6 knots, a pleasant "nice but easy" breeze, gusting occasionally to around 12–13. The **best window to be out is roughly noon to 4 PM**, when the southerly is most established. You'll find a knot or two more pressure the farther **south and seaward** you go — off the south side of Hog Island and out toward the East Passage — while the **inner harbor stays the lightest**. Expect a soft, shadowed patch tucked in behind **Poppasquash Point (the west shore)**, so if you go glassy there, nudge back out toward open water to find the breeze again. Models are in good agreement on the light-air story, so plan for a calm day.

## Tide & current
High water is at **12:53 PM** (about 3.8 ft) and the next low is at **6:18 PM** (about 0.8 ft), so the tide is dropping through your whole afternoon. With low water arriving right at the end of the day, give the **shallow harbor edges and shoals** a bit of extra room as evening approaches. Current is modest and mostly a non-issue for a casual sail: at the harbor mouth off Hog Island a gentle flood runs up-bay until about **2 PM**, then it turns to a light ebb setting seaward. Out in the **East Passage** the flow is a touch stronger (up to about a knot), ebbing **south/seaward** after roughly 3:45 PM — so late in the day, with the southerly breeze blowing against that outgoing current, you might notice a little short chop building out in the Passage. Nothing alarming in this light air, just a heads-up so it doesn't surprise you.

## Good to know
Comfortable summer conditions — highs around **80°F**, lows near 75, with a mix of **mostly cloudy and partly sunny** skies. There's only a **slight chance of a passing shower** (under 20%), so keep an eye up but don't let it stop you. Bring sunscreen and water even under the clouds, and a light layer in case the sea breeze feels cool on the water. With winds this gentle it's an easy, forgiving day, but always keep life jackets handy. A nice plan: **head out around late morning** to catch the sea breeze as it fills, enjoy the heart of the afternoon, and aim to be **back by around 5–5:30 PM** ahead of that evening low water and the fading breeze. Have a great sail!
