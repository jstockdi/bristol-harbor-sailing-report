## Tactical call
**Course-agnostic:** The lever tonight is **pressure geography, not current**. The breeze is a steady **S/SSW**, stronger along the **western Poppasquash shore and out in the East Passage**, with a **soft lee draped over the harbor mouth and immediately south of Hog Island**. Whatever the beat, **stay connected to the pressure bands off Poppasquash and in the Passage and avoid parking in Hog Island's wind shadow.** Current is near-slack for the first 40 minutes, then becomes a **weak NNE flood** — a small bonus for legs heading **N/up-bay** late in the window, a small brake going **S/seaward** after ~18:45. With a S wind and a building N flood, expect **wind-against-tide chop** to start stacking up in the Passage channel in the back half. And with dead low at the gun, **keep clear of the shoal edges early.**

**If the course is set:**
- **N–S windward/leeward:** The E–W pressure gradient is your cross-course lever — the **west/Poppasquash side carries more breeze** than the harbor-mouth notch. Late in the window the NNE flood nudges the northbound (up-bay) beat; a S-bound leg pays a small current tax after 18:45.
- **J-hook out of the harbor:** Start in near-slack inner-harbor water, but mind the **thin water at low tide**. Once you hook out toward the mouth you cross into the Hog Island soft patch — **don't stall there**; press through to the Passage pressure.
- **Around Hog Island:** Each leg gets its own call. The **NW/mouth quadrant is the light spot**; the **west shore and Passage side carry the breeze**. Current reverses to a weak NNE flood mid-lap, so a late up-bay leg gets a push while the early seaward leg fights a dying ebb/near-slack.
- **Out-and-back (light air):** Pressure dominates — reach out toward the **East Passage breeze**, but don't overstand into Hog Island's lee on the way. Coming back N, the building flood is with you.
