## The day
This is a mellow, easygoing day on the water — perfect for a relaxed family daysail rather than a spirited rail-down romp. Skies are sunny and warm, the water's calm, and the breeze is on the light side, so think leisurely reaching and lots of time to enjoy the harbor. Mornings will be near-glassy, but a light southerly sea breeze should build through the afternoon to give you a proper, if gentle, sail. Bring patience early and you'll be rewarded later.

## Wind
Expect a slow start: the early hours are barely a whisper, with the live anemometers at Newport and Conimicut reading just 1–3 knots out of the NW/WSW — true drifter conditions if you head out at the 10 AM bell. As the day warms, the classic Narragansett sea breeze should swing around and fill from the **S/SSW**, coming up the East Passage and around Hog Island. Through the afternoon look for a soft-but-sailable **5–7 knots**, with the odd puff nudging into the low teens — enough to keep things moving and put a nice heel on without ever feeting overpowered. The **best window is roughly 1–5 PM**, once that southerly is established. Expect a light patch tucked in behind **Poppasquash Neck (the western shore)** — if you find yourself parked over there, ease out toward the middle of the harbor or down toward **Hog Island** to the south, where there's usually a touch more pressure. Forecast confidence is good; the models are in close agreement.

## Tide & current
Low water was early (around 7 AM at just 0.8 ft), so the tide is **rising all morning to a high of about 3.3 ft at 2:34 PM**, then ebbing away toward the next low near 8:51 PM. That means plenty of water under you through the prime afternoon window — nice for poking around the harbor edges — though it's still worth giving the shallow spots near the shoreline and the Hog Island shoals a respectful berth as the tide drops later on. Current is very gentle inside the harbor: the flow near the **harbor mouth** turns to a mild flood (setting NNE, up into the harbor) around lunchtime and drifts back to slack near 3:50 PM. Out in the **East Passage** it runs a bit firmer (up to about 0.8 kt), so you'll feel it more if you venture out past Hog Island. With winds this light there's no meaningful wind-against-tide chop to worry about — the water should stay friendly and smooth.

## Good to know
It's a lovely late-summer day: **sunny to mostly sunny, 77–82°F**, and only an **11% rain chance** — no thunderstorm worries on the board. Bring sunscreen, a hat, and plenty of water, because that sun and heat will find you on a slow-moving boat. The breeze stays light enough that this is a comfortable, low-stress outing, but keep life jackets handy for the kids and anyone who wants them, especially if an afternoon puff pipes up into the teens. A relaxed plan: head out late morning knowing the first hour or two may be ghosting-along light, enjoy the fill through early-to-mid afternoon, and aim to be back at the dock by early evening as the breeze softens and the tide starts to ebb. Easy day — go enjoy it.
