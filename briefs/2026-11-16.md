## The day
This is a gentle, low-key day on the water — think a quiet November cruise rather than a thrilling blast. A light northwesterly is all that's on offer (mostly 4–6 kt, with the odd puff near the harbor mouth), so you'll be ghosting along more than heeling over. Pair that with wall-to-wall sunshine, flat water, and no rain in sight, and it's a lovely afternoon for a relaxed sail if you're happy to go slow. Just dress warm — the air is cool and the breeze is light enough that boat speed won't make its own wind.

## Wind
Expect a soft northwesterly all day, roughly 4–6 kt with gusts touching 9–12 kt out toward Hog Island and the East Passage mouth. The forecast actually looks a touch optimistic — the live anemometers up and down the Passage are reading a bit lighter (Conimicut around 5 kt, Newport only 3–4 and wandering in direction), so plan for the softer end of that range. In plain terms: it's a **drifter-to-light day**, perfect for a gentle sail but not one that'll power you up.

Best window is **late morning into early afternoon**, when the breeze is at its steadiest before it fades toward sunset. The more open water near the harbor mouth and out toward the East Passage will hold a knot or two more than the inner harbor. Watch for soft, glassy patches in the lee of the Poppasquash shore to the west — with a NW wind coming off that land, you'll find holes and fluky puffs tucked in close under the point, so give yourself a little room off that shore if you want to keep moving.

## Tide & current

Current is light inside the harbor and easy to ignore for a casual sail. Out at the harbor mouth off Hog Island it's gentle — a weak flood easing to slack around **1:52 PM**, then a soft ebb (set to the SSW, down-bay) building into the evening. In the East Passage the ebb has a little more teeth (up toward 0.9 kt after the 3:29 PM slack). With the wind out of the NW and the ebb setting SSW, there's a mild wind-against-current angle out in the Passage, but at these breeze strengths it won't kick up anything worse than a light ripple. Nothing to worry a casual sailor.

## Good to know
Cool, bright, and dry — highs around 53°F, dropping toward the mid-40s, with sunny-to-partly-cloudy skies and zero rain chance. Dress for November: layers, a warm hat, and gloves will make the difference between a pleasant sail and a chilly one, since the light air won't warm you up. The sun sits low this time of year and sets early, so plan to be **out by late morning and back by mid-afternoon** to make the most of the steadier breeze and the daylight. Conditions are calm enough that this is an easy, low-stress outing — but with cold water this time of year, wear your life jacket and let someone ashore know your plan. Enjoy the quiet.
