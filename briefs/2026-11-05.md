## The day
This is a genuinely good November sailing day — surprisingly mild (mid-60s °F) with a steady, spirited SW breeze that's already up and working. It's not a lazy drifter; think 10–16 knots with gusts nudging the low 20s, so you'll be powered up and moving nicely. Crews who enjoy an honest breeze will love it; if you're taking out little ones or a tender boat, plan to tuck in a reef and keep it mellow. The main caveat is the sky: it's cloudy with a rain chance that climbs as the day goes on, so this is a "go earlier, enjoy it, be home before the wet stuff" kind of day.

## Wind
The breeze is in and holding from the **S/SW** all through the window — no waiting around for it to fill. The live anemometers back up the forecast nicely: Newport (out at the Passage mouth) is reading around 13 kt gusting 15, and Conimicut up the bay is a touch friskier at 16 gusting into the low 20s, so expect the stronger puffs to feel real. Forecasts and the real-world readings agree well, so you can trust this one. Across the harbor and out toward Hog Island it's pretty even — roughly 10–11 kt everywhere — nicely powered-up sailing rather than a handful.

The one soft spot to expect is tucked in close under the **Poppasquash shore to the west**, where the land takes the edge off the breeze and it'll feel lighter and a little patchy; sail out a bit from that shore to stay in clean, steady wind. **Best window** is late morning through mid-afternoon while the breeze is steady and the sky is at its driest.

The tide then rises steadily to a **high around 16:47**, so depth is on your side through the afternoon. (The next low isn't until 22:47.)

Through the afternoon it's **flooding up-bay (toward the NNE, into the harbor)**, which runs roughly with the SW wind — so no nasty wind-against-tide chop to surprise you. Seas are a comfortable 2–3 ft. Easy, friendly conditions on the water surface.

## Good to know
Mild for the season — mid-60s °F — but it's **mostly cloudy with a real chance of rain** that increases later in the day (and patchy fog possible toward evening), so pack a layer and foulies and keep an eye to windward. With gusts reaching the low 20s, **life jackets on for everyone** is the smart call, especially with cooler November water. A good plan: **head out late morning to early afternoon**, enjoy the steady breeze, and aim to be **back at the dock by mid-to-late afternoon** before the rain and reduced visibility settle in. Dress warm, keep it fun, and you've got a lovely autumn sail ahead.
