## The day
This is a genuinely nice day to go sailing — a mellow-to-pleasant family daysail with just enough breeze to keep things fun. A steady southerly sea breeze of around 7–9 knots is filling in over warm, mostly sunny skies in the low 70s. It's not a drifter and it's not a handful; it's the kind of gentle powered-up sailing where you can relax, share the tiller, and enjoy being on the water. The occasional gust into the mid-teens will give you a brief lively moment, so keep a hand ready, but nothing to worry about.

## Wind
The breeze is a classic southerly, coming up the bay from the S/SSW and building through late morning into the afternoon. Expect roughly **7–9 knots** across the harbor and out toward Hog Island, nudging a touch stronger — call it 8–9 — as you head south toward the harbor mouth and the East Passage, where it's more open to the sea breeze. Gusts can flick up to the mid-to-high teens, so nothing extreme but enough for a fun heel now and then. The models are in tight agreement on this one (very small spread), and the live anemometers are actually reading a bit **stronger** than the forecast suggested this morning — so if anything, lean toward the fuller end of that range.

Best window is the **afternoon**, roughly noon through 5 pm, when the sea breeze is most established and reliable. The one soft spot to expect is tucked in behind **Poppasquash Neck (the west shore)** — as with any southerly-ish flow, you'll find lighter, patchier air in the lee close to that shore, so stay a little off it if you want steadier pressure. Out in the more open water toward Hog Island and beyond, the breeze is smoother and a hair stronger.

## Tide & current
High tide is right in the middle of your day at **1:03 pm (about 3.3 ft)**, with low water bookending things — an early low at 5:27 am and the next low at **6:27 pm (about 1.0 ft)**. That means water is dropping through the late afternoon, so if you're poking around the shallow edges near the harbor's shoreline late in the day, give them extra room as the tide runs out.

Current inside Bristol Harbor is gentle and won't bother you. Out at the **harbor mouth by Hog Island** it's light (well under half a knot), turning to a mild ebb (running SSW, seaward) after about 4:20 pm. The **East Passage** runs noticeably stronger — up to roughly ¾–1 knot — with the ebb building there from around 4 pm. Here's the one heads-up: that afternoon ebb flows **south/seaward** while the wind blows **from the south**, so you'll get a bit of **wind-against-tide chop** out in the Passage later in the day. Nothing dangerous in this breeze, just a short, bouncier sea state out there after ~4 pm — worth knowing if you've got kids or newer crew aboard.

## Good to know
It's a warm, pleasant early-fall day: highs around **75°F**, lows near 71°F, mostly-to-partly sunny skies, and only a **9% chance of rain** — essentially a dry day with no thunderstorm concern. Bring sunscreen, water, and a light layer for when the sea breeze cools things off. A great plan is to **head out around late morning or noon** to catch the building breeze at high water, enjoy the afternoon, and aim to be **back by around 5–5:30 pm** — that keeps you ahead of the dropping tide and the choppier ebb out in the Passage. Life jackets on for kids as always, and it's smart to have everyone's within reach given those occasional mid-teens gusts. Enjoy it — this is a beautiful September afternoon to be sailing.
