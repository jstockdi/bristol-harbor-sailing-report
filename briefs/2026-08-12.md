## The day
This is a lovely, low-stress day to be on the water — think mellow family daysail, not a spirited rail-down romp. Skies are sunny, air's warm in the low 80s, and the breeze stays light all day (averaging around 5 knots with the odd puff into the low teens). Nobody's getting overpowered here; if anything the challenge is finding enough wind, so bring patience, sunscreen, and maybe a book for the drifty spells.

## Wind
Expect a light **northwesterly** to start the morning — the live anemometers at Newport and Conimicut are both reading only 3–4 kt out of the NW around 7:30, so it's genuinely soft early on. Through the middle of the day the classic **SW sea breeze** should nose in up the East Passage past Hog Island and swing the wind around to the **south-southwest** by afternoon. Even at its best it's a gentle 5–6 kt with gusts to maybe 10–13 out toward Hog Island and the East Passage — a pleasant, easy sailing breeze rather than anything you'd reef for. The models are actually a touch optimistic (the real obs have been running lighter than forecast), so plan for the low end and treat any extra as a bonus. Your **best window is early-to-mid afternoon**, roughly 1–4 PM, once that sea breeze has filled. Soft spots to expect: it's always lightest tucked up inside the harbor and in the **lee just east/southeast of the Poppasquash shore**, so if you go looking for what little pressure there is, you'll find a bit more of it out toward the harbor mouth and south of Hog Island.

## Tide & current
High water is early, around **08:00** (about 4.3 ft), then it drops to a notably **low tide at 1:33 PM** — and it goes slightly negative (about −0.2 ft), so give the shallow harbor edges and any skinny water off the points a wide berth in the early afternoon. It builds back to another high around **8:26 PM**. Current is mild at the harbor mouth: a weak SSW ebb through late morning, slack around **1:23 PM**, then a gentle flood building back into the harbor in the evening. Out in the **East Passage** the flow is a bit stronger (up to about a knot on the late-morning ebb, slack near 2:35 PM), so if you venture out there mid-morning you'll feel it setting you seaward. With the wind this light there's no meaningful wind-against-tide chop to worry about — it should stay comfortable and flat.

## Good to know
Just about a perfect summer weather day: **sunny to mostly clear, 77–82°F, and only ~9% chance of rain** — no thunderstorm concerns on the horizon. The main thing to manage is the **sun and heat**, so hat, sunscreen, water, and a light layer for the breeze. Conditions are gentle enough that this is an easy, relaxed sail, but always have life jackets aboard and handy. A nice plan: head out around midday to catch the sea breeze as it builds, enjoy the afternoon, and aim to be back before the wind fades toward evening — no rush, no drama.
