## The day
This is a calm, pretty late-October day on the water — a proper mellow family daysail rather than anything spirited. Clear skies, light northeasterly breeze in the 5–6 kt range with the odd puff into the low teens, and flat little waves around a foot or two. Nobody's getting overpowered; the main question is whether there's *enough* wind for your boat. For a relaxed cruise with hot cocoa aboard, it's lovely. If you want to feel the boat heel and move, you'll be hunting the puffs and the slightly breezier water down toward the harbor mouth.

## Wind
Expect a soft, steady northeasterly most of the day — drifter-to-light-air sailing. Forecast averages sit around 5 kt across the harbor, nudging a touch stronger (closer to 6 kt) as you get down toward Hog Island Shoal and out into the East Passage, with gusts reaching the low teens when a puff rolls through. The live anemometers back this up: Newport and Conimicut Light are both reading 7–8 kt midday with peaks near 10–11, and the models broadly agree (confidence is good here), so no nasty surprises hiding. Your best air is late morning through mid-afternoon. The soft spots: tuck too far under the **Poppasquash shore to the west** and you'll find a wind shadow and some glassy patches — stay out toward the middle of the harbor and toward **Hog Island to the south** to keep the sails full.

## Tide & current
High water was around 09:00 and there's a low around 15:00 (and it's a genuinely low one, right around the chart datum), so be mindful of the shallow edges near the harbor shores and any thin water off the points through early-to-mid afternoon — give the fringes a wide berth. Current is gentle inside the harbor and near slack around 10:00, then a modest ebb (sliding **SSW, down-bay toward the sea**) builds through midday before easing to slack again near 14:00. Out in the **East Passage** the ebb runs a bit harder (over a knot), so if you poke out past Hog Island you'll feel more set there. With the light NE breeze over an ebbing (southbound) current you may notice a touch of short, bumpy chop at the harbor mouth around midday — nothing dramatic, just a heads-up so it doesn't catch you off guard.

## Good to know
Dress for autumn: highs only in the low-to-mid 50s and dipping toward the upper 40s, so layers, a warm hat, and something windproof will make all the difference out on the cool water — it'll feel chillier under sail than on the dock. The upside is a beautiful **sunny, clear day with no rain** in the forecast. Sun's lower this time of year, so you may want sunglasses but won't be fighting much heat. A good plan: head out around 10:30–11:00 to catch the best of the breeze and the slack-to-gentle current, and aim to be back at the dock by mid-afternoon before the light fades and the air cools off. It's light enough that you won't be fighting the boat, but keep life jackets handy for the puffy gusts and for anyone who chills easily. Enjoy the quiet end-of-season sail!
