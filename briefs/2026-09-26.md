## The day
Honestly, this is a day to be cautious about. A firm NNE wind is already blowing 18–22 knots out on the water this morning, and the forecast keeps it powered-up all day — plus there's a real chance of rain showers, fog, and even thunderstorms rolling through. On its own the breeze would be a spirited, reef-in ride for experienced hands; paired with the storm risk and cool, damp air, it tips into marginal. If you're looking for a mellow family sail, this isn't it — better to wait for a kinder day. If you do go, keep it short, keep it local, and keep a close eye on the sky.

## Wind
The wind is up from the north-northeast and staying up — expect a solid 16–21 knots across the harbor and mouth through most of the day, with the models in good agreement (and the live anemometers at Newport and Conimicut backing them up, so this is a confident call, not a guess). That's firmly reef-it territory for most boats, and there are gustier punches possible with any passing shower, so don't get caught over-canvassed. Being a northerly, it'll be shifty and puffy coming off the Bristol and Poppasquash land — expect the breeze to come and go in strength rather than sit steady. The one softer pocket is tucked in behind Poppasquash Point, on its east-to-southeast side, where the land throws a lee; nice if you want a breather, but the wind will fill back in hard as you clear the shadow. If you sail at all, the least-bad window is late morning to early afternoon, before any afternoon storms build.

## Tide & current
High water was around 8:00 this morning, with low water near 1:50 PM — and that low sits right down at chart datum, so give the shallow edges of the harbor a wide berth around midday. Current at the harbor mouth is ebbing (running seaward to the south-southwest) through the early afternoon, going slack around 1:25 PM before the flood builds later toward evening. Out in the East Passage the flow is much stronger — over a knot on the ebb. Good news for comfort: through the morning and midday the ebb runs the same way the wind is blowing, which actually keeps the chop down. The thing to watch is late afternoon — once the flood turns and starts pushing north against that northerly wind, the harbor mouth and Passage will kick up a short, steep chop. Plan to be back before that sets in.

## Good to know
It's a cool, raw day — air only around 59–60°F, with rain showers, patchy fog, and a high chance (over 90%) of showers and thunderstorms. **If there's any thunder or lightning in the area, stay ashore — no sail is worth it.** Dress warm and waterproof; visibility could drop in the fog. Given the wind strength, life jackets on for everyone, all the time, and don't sail solo. If you decide the window looks clear enough, head out mid-to-late morning while the ebb keeps things flatter, stay inside the harbor or close to it, and aim to be tied up well before early afternoon when storms and the wind-against-tide chop both become more likely. When in doubt, this is a fine day to enjoy the harbor from the dock.
