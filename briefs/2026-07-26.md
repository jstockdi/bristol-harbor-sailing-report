## The day
This is a proper feel-good daysail. A steady northerly of about 9–10 kt, gusting into the low teens, gives you enough to move nicely without ever feeling like a handful — a mellow, comfortable breeze for family sailing or a relaxed cruise. Add near-total sunshine and comfortable temperatures and you've got one of those easy summer days where the hardest decision is when to turn around. Nothing marginal here: not a drifter, not a reef-it day, just nice.

## Wind
The breeze is already up this morning — the real anemometers at Newport and Conimicut are reading a steady 8–9 kt out of the N/NNE, and the forecast agrees, so you can trust it. Expect that same **N/NNE flow near 9–10 kt** to hold through the day, with gusts to around 14–15 kt, easing back a touch toward evening. It stays a pleasant, powered-but-relaxed strength the whole window. One thing about a northerly here: because it's blowing off the land it can be a little **puffy and shifty**, so stay alert on the tiller for small headers and lifts. The classic soft spot to expect is **tucked in behind Poppasquash Neck on its E/SE side**, where the land throws a lee — if you sail into a hole there, nudge out toward more open water and the breeze fills back in. Out toward the harbor mouth by Hog Island and into the East Passage you'll generally find the most consistent air. **Best window: late morning through mid-afternoon**, roughly 10:30 to 15:30, while the breeze is at its steadiest.

## Tide & current
Low water lands right around **11:32 (about 0.6 ft)** and it's a proper high afterward at **18:52 (3.5 ft)**. That midday low is the thing to keep in mind — give the **shallow edges near the harbor shores and any thin spots around the flats a wide berth** in the late morning; you'll have plenty more water under you by mid-afternoon as the tide floods back in. Current is gentle at the harbor mouth (well under half a knot), running seaward on a light ebb early, then near-slack around midday before a soft afternoon flood back up-bay. It runs a bit stronger out in the **East Passage** — up to roughly ¾–1 kt — where it ebbs seaward until slack around 13:00, then floods. With the wind out of the N, the morning ebb runs the same way as the breeze so the water stays smooth; once the afternoon flood sets up-bay against the northerly you may notice a little **light chop near the harbor mouth and in the Passage** — nothing dramatic, just a heads-up so it doesn't surprise you.

## Good to know
Gorgeous conditions: **sunny and clear all day**, highs around 76°F and comfortable, with essentially **no rain** (1% chance) and no storm concerns. Do bring the usual summer kit — sunscreen, hat, water — because clear skies and sun on the water add up fast. The breeze is friendly, but with gusts into the mid-teens it's still worth having **life jackets on the younger crew** and within easy reach for everyone, especially out toward the more open water past Hog Island. Head out mid-to-late morning to catch the best of the breeze, and aim to be back before evening as the wind softens and the tide floods up — a relaxed, easy day on the water.
