## The day
Honestly, this is a mellow-to-marginal one — lovely and mild for late November (near 60°F), but the breeze is feather-light all day. If your idea of a good sail is a quiet, unhurried ghost around the harbor with the kettle on when you get back, you'll enjoy it. If you want to actually go somewhere, you may spend more time drifting than sailing. Add a real chance of rain building through the afternoon and the move is clear: go early, stay close, and treat it as a gentle shakeout rather than an adventure.

## Wind
Light and from the **east/ESE** most of the day — think 3–6 kt with the odd little puff to 10–12. That's drifter-to-barely-ghosting territory; a light-air boat or a big genoa will thank you. The live anemometer down at Newport is reading a modest 4–5 kt from the ESE, while up-bay at Conimicut it's nearly flat calm — a good sign that it stays soft and patchy inside the harbor. Models and the real wind are in close agreement, so don't expect a hidden sea breeze to save the day; this easterly won't build into anything until well after dark. Your best shot at steady air is the open water toward the **harbor mouth and south of Hog Island**, where there's a touch more fetch. Expect dead patches tucked under the **Poppasquash shore to the west** — if you drift in there, be patient or paddle out toward the middle.

## Tide & current
Low water lands right in the middle of your morning at **11:21** — and it's a big minus tide (essentially bottom-of-the-barrel), so give the shallow edges and the harbor fringes a wide berth and don't cut corners near shore or the flats around Hog Island Shoal. High tides bracket the day at **05:11** and **17:37**. Current is gentle and friendly for a casual sail: near slack at the harbor mouth around **10:33**, then a light flood building up-bay (NNE) through the afternoon. The East Passage runs a bit harder — ebbing seaward until about **11:45**, then flooding back up-bay after ~15:24 — but with this little wind there's no nasty wind-against-tide chop to worry about. Just know that in such light air the current can nudge you around more than the breeze does, so keep an eye on your drift.

## Good to know
Mild, soft, and grey: temperatures in the **high 50s to low 60s**, cloudy skies, and a growing **chance of light rain (around 60%)** through the afternoon — with steadier rain and much stronger easterlies arriving tonight. That evening system is the thing to stay ahead of. Dress for November on the water anyway (the air is kind but the water is cold now), bring a layer you don't mind getting damp, and wear your life jacket as a matter of course — cold-water season, even on a gentle day. Simple plan: **head out late morning around 10:30–11:00** to catch what little breeze there is and slack current, and **be back at the dock by mid-afternoon (2–3 PM)** before the rain and the building easterly roll in.
