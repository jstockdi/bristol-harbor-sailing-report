## The day
This is a really nice one to get out on — a proper sailing day with enough breeze to feel alive without being a handful. A steady southerly runs 10–12 knots through the afternoon, gusting into the mid-to-high teens (a bit more out in the open water), so it's a spirited daysail rather than a mellow drifter. If you like a boat that's heeled and pulling, you'll love it; if you've got kids or newer crew aboard, tuck in a reef and pick the calmer inner-harbor water and it's still very manageable. All three forecast models agree almost exactly, and the live anemometers back it up, so you can trust the picture.

## Wind
The wind is out of the **south to south-southwest** all day — a classic sea-breeze setup blowing up the East Passage and over Hog Island into the harbor. Expect a comfortable **10–12 kt** with **gusts around 17–20**, which puts you in the nice-to-powered-up range: enough to trim in and lean on the rail. Right now the real anemometers tell the story — Newport at the Passage mouth is reading about 10 kt, while Conimicut up the bay is already puffing 16 gusting 18, so there's a touch more muscle the further out and up-bay you go. The **strongest, steadiest breeze sits out toward Hog Island and the East Passage**; the softest patch, as always, will be tucked in the **lee off the western Poppasquash shore**, where the land blocks the southerly — expect a light, fluky pocket if you sail up under that side. Best window to be out is **midday through late afternoon**, when the sea breeze is fully established and even.

## Tide & current
High water was at **07:53**, then a big drainout to a **very low tide at 13:31 — just 0.2 ft**, with the next high at **20:09**. That midday low is worth respecting: give the **shallow harbor edges and any thin spots near shore a wide berth** around lunchtime, and don't cut corners on the flats. On the water, the current ebbs (runs seaward, SSW) from late morning until it goes slack around **13:30–14:40**, then turns to flood. Because that seaward ebb runs against the southerly breeze, you'll get some **short, choppy wind-against-tide slop late morning into early afternoon, especially out in the East Passage** where the current is strongest (over a knot near Dyer Island). Nothing dangerous, but a casual sailor will notice the bumpier water out there — the inner harbor stays gentler.

## Good to know
Warm and pleasant — highs around **78°F**, partly sunny skies, and only a slim **~26% chance of a passing shower**, so no real thunderstorm worry, but glance at the sky and don't be shocked by a stray sprinkle. Bring sun protection and water; late-August sun is still strong. With gusts reaching the high teens it's a **life-jackets-on** kind of day, particularly for kids and anyone in a smaller or tippier boat. A good plan: **head out mid-to-late morning** to enjoy the building breeze, keep an eye on that choppy midday water out toward the Passage, and aim to be **back by late afternoon** while the wind is still friendly and well before evening. Have fun out there.
