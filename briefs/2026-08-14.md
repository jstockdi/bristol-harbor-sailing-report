## The day
This is a lovely, low-key day to get out — think mellow family daysail rather than a spirited rail-down affair. Breeze is on the light side (averaging 5–6 kt) with warm, largely sunny skies in the mid-to-upper 70s. Nothing to overpower you; just enough to move nicely once the afternoon sea breeze fills. The one thing to keep half an eye on is the chance of an isolated shower or thunderstorm bubbling up — more on that below.

## Wind
The morning starts with a light **northerly** (roughly N–NNW, ~345°) drifting down off the Bristol and Poppasquash land. It's gentle — 5 kt or so inside the harbor, a touch more out toward the East Passage — and being an off-the-land breeze it'll be a bit shifty and puffy, so expect to trim actively. Live buoys this morning back that up: Newport is reading around 5–8 kt N, with Conimicut a little friskier up-bay near 9–13 kt.

Through the middle of the day the wind swings all the way around to the **south** as the sea breeze takes over, filling up the East Passage from the S/SSW. By afternoon you've got a steadier, friendlier ~5–6 kt southerly — a proper "nice sailing" breeze that won't push you around, with only the odd gust toward the low teens (mostly if a shower passes nearby). Models are in tight agreement, so this is a confident, easy picture.

**Best window: early-to-mid afternoon**, once that southerly settles in. Expect a **soft patch tucked under the Poppasquash shore** — in the morning northerly its lee sits to the E/SE, and under the afternoon southerly the western Poppasquash edge goes light — so if you fall into a hole there, nudge out toward more open water to find pressure.

## Tide & current
High water is early, at **09:38 (4.5 ft)**, then it drains to a **low at 15:19 (essentially 0 ft / a hair below)** before filling again toward evening. That afternoon low means **thin water along the harbor edges and over the shallow spots near the shores** — give the margins a wide berth and stay in deeper water if you draw much.

Current is gentle right at the harbor mouth (slack around 10:56, then a light ebb setting SSW/seaward through the early afternoon, slack again near 15:00). Out in the **East Passage** it runs harder — the midday ebb can reach about 1 kt heading down-bay. Here's the casual-sailor heads-up: once the **southerly sea breeze meets that seaward-running ebb** in the early-to-mid afternoon (roughly 13:00–15:00), you can get a bit of short, choppy slop where the two meet near the harbor mouth and out toward the Passage. Nothing dramatic in this light air, just don't be surprised by a lumpier ride out there.

## Good to know
Warm and comfortable — **74–80°F**, mostly sunny, an easy day for shorts-and-sunscreen sailing. Bring water, a hat, and sun protection; the light air won't cool you much. The catch is a **28% chance of isolated-to-scattered showers and thunderstorms** — typical summer pop-up stuff. Keep an eye to the west/northwest for building clouds, and if you hear thunder or see a dark cell forming, head in early; storm gusts can arrive fast even on a light day, so have life jackets handy and on the kids.

Easy plan: **head out late morning to early afternoon** to catch the sea breeze at its best, and aim to be **back by mid-to-late afternoon** — that keeps you ahead of the lowest water and the peak thunderstorm window. Enjoy it out there.
