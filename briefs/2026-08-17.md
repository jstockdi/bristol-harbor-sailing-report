## The day
This is a nice, low-key day to get out on the water — call it a mellow family daysail with a bit of extra life in the puffs. The breeze is a moderate SE-to-ESE running in the single digits with occasional gusts into the mid-teens, so nothing intimidating, just enough to keep the boat moving and put a smile on your face. The one caveat is the sky: showers are likely and there's some patchy fog around, so this is a "watch the clouds and go when it's clear" kind of day rather than a bluebird one. Cooler air, too — pack a layer.

## Wind
Expect a steady, friendly breeze of roughly **6–8 knots most of the day**, out of the **southeast early, gradually backing toward the east-southeast** through the afternoon. That's comfortable, easy sailing — nice-breeze territory, with the odd gust to the mid-teens that'll give you a fun little lean before it eases off again. Worth noting: the real anemometers this morning are reading **stronger than the forecast** (Conimicut up the bay was averaging around 13 knots with gusts to 17), so don't be surprised if it feels a touch more powered-up than "6 knots" suggests — a genuinely spirited sail is on the table if that trend holds. Models are in tight agreement on direction and strength, so the picture is reliable.

Best window to be out is **late morning through the early afternoon**, when the breeze is most established. The usual soft spot applies: tuck in close under **Poppasquash Neck (the west shore)** and you'll find the wind goes light and patchy in its lee — expect it to fill back in and shift as you clear the shadow and get back into open water toward Hog Island.

## Tide & current
High water is right around **midday (12:04, ~4.1 ft)**, with lows at **04:49** (before you're out) and again at **17:30 (0.6 ft)**. That late-afternoon low is the one to watch — if you're poking around the shallow edges near the harbor rim or the flats late in the day, give them extra room, as there won't be much water under the keel.

Current is gentle inside the harbor and picks up at the **harbor mouth (Hog Island Shoal)** and out in the **East Passage**. Through the morning it's flooding (running up-bay, into the harbor); it goes slack near **1:00 pm** and then ebbs (running seaward, SSW) into the mid-afternoon — the ebb is noticeably stronger out in the East Passage (around a knot). With the SE-ish wind pushing against that afternoon ebb out toward Hog Island and the Passage, you may notice a **short, choppy little sea building out there** in the afternoon — nothing dramatic, but a heads-up so it doesn't catch you off guard. Inside the harbor it stays easy.

## Good to know
Cool and comfortable, with highs only around **71–75°F** — bring a jacket or spray top, it won't be a shorts-and-sunscreen scorcher. The main thing to plan around is the weather: **rain showers are likely** (up to ~60% chance) and there's **patchy fog** in the mix, so visibility could drop. Head out **mid-morning** to catch the best of the breeze and try to be **back before the late-afternoon low tide and any building showers** — a return by early-to-mid afternoon is the smart play. If the fog rolls in, stay close to home and keep track of your bearings. With gusts reaching the mid-teens, keep **life jackets on** for kids and anyone less steady, and reef down early if it feels like more than you bargained for. Have a great sail!
