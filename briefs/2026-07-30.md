## The day
This is a good day to get out — just dress for it. A steady **northerly (NNE)** breeze of around **9–10 knots** holds through the whole window, which makes for enjoyable, honest sailing: enough to move nicely without being a handful. The catch isn't the wind, it's the sky — it's cool (upper 60s), damp, and there's patchy fog with a real chance of showers. Think of it as a comfortable daysail for anyone happy in a jacket, with a couple of gustier moments to keep it interesting. Families and casual crews will be fine; just keep an eye on the fog and know where you are.

## Wind
The breeze is out of the **north-northeast** and stays remarkably consistent all day — no waiting around for it to fill. Expect a **pleasant 9–10 knots** across most of the harbor, a touch more (10–11) out toward **Hog Island Shoal and the East Passage**, and puffs into the **high teens / around 20 knots**, so there's the odd powered-up gust worth being ready for. The live anemometers at Newport and up at Conimicut are both reading NNE in the 8–11 knot range this morning, right in line with the forecast, so confidence is good.

Because it's a northerly blowing off the Bristol and Poppasquash land, expect it to be a little **shifty and puffy** rather than glassy-steady — fun, but stay alert on the sheet. The reliable **soft spot** is tucked in behind **Poppasquash Neck**, to the east and southeast of that shore, where the land throws a wind shadow; you'll feel it go light there and firm back up as you clear out into open water. Inside the inner harbor is also a hair lighter than out by the island. Best sailing is anywhere from the harbor mouth out toward Hog Island where the breeze is most honest.

## Tide & current
**High water is early, at 9:02 AM**, then it drains to a **low of about 0.3 ft at 2:33 PM** before filling again toward the 9:16 PM high. That afternoon low is genuinely low, so **give the shallow harbor edges and any thin spots near the shore a wide berth** in the early-to-mid afternoon.

Current-wise, things are near-**slack around mid-morning** (about 10:15–10:35), then the tide **ebbs seaward (SSW, down-bay) through midday**, running hardest around 12–1 PM — up over **1 knot in the East Passage** off Dyer Island. Here's the one heads-up: with the wind out of the north and the ebb setting south against it, you'll get some **wind-against-tide chop at the harbor mouth and out in the Passage roughly noon to 2:30 PM**. Nothing alarming, but a casual sailor shouldn't be surprised by a short, bouncy sea out there. It eases back to slack around 2:30–3:45 PM. If you'd rather keep it smooth, sail inside the harbor during that midday stretch and save the open-water reaching for later.

## Good to know
It's a **cool, gray one** — highs only around **68–71°F**, with **patchy fog and a good chance (up to ~68%) of showers**. Bring **warm layers and foul-weather gear**, and don't count on much sun to dry you out. The fog is the thing to respect: keep your visibility in mind, carry a way to signal, and don't wander far offshore if it thickens. With gusts flirting with 20 knots, this is a **life-jackets-on** kind of day, especially for kids and anyone in a small boat.

A nice plan: **head out late morning** once the current goes slack (around 10:30) for the smoothest start, enjoy the steady breeze, and **be back by mid-to-late afternoon** — both to dodge the worst of the midday chop and to stay ahead of any showers rolling through. If the fog closes in, there's no shame in cutting it short and calling it a good sail.
