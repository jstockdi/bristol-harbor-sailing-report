## The day
This is a sporty autumn day rather than a lazy one. A steady northerly is blowing down the bay in the low-to-mid teens with gusts into the low 20s — enough to put a grin on your face and a bit of heel in the boat, but on the powered-up side for a relaxed family outing. Add cool air in the upper 40s and it's a dress-warm, pick-your-moment kind of sail. If you and your crew like a little zip and you bundle up, you'll have a great time; if you were hoping to drift and sunbathe, this isn't that day.

## Wind
The breeze is already in and it's staying — a northerly (roughly out of the N, 350–360°) holding all day with no real lull to wait for. Inside the harbor expect a comfortable 11–12 kt, nicely powered-up; head out toward the harbor mouth off Hog Island and into the East Passage and it builds to 15–16 kt with gusts near 22. Being a northerly straight off the land to the N, it'll be **shifty and puffy** — expect the odd gust that heels you and then eases, so keep a hand ready on the sheet. The classic soft spot is in the lee **E/SE of Poppasquash Neck** — tuck in close under that western shore and you'll find flatter water and lighter air if you want a breather. The live anemometers back the forecast nicely (Conimicut up the bay is reading ~15 kt, Newport near the Passage ~10–12), so confidence is good. Best window is **roughly noon to 4 PM**, after any morning sprinkle clears and before the water gets lumpier late.

## Tide & current
High water is right in the middle of your day at **1:13 PM** (about 3 ft), with the next low out at **6:52 PM** (just over 1 ft). That evening low is near the end of the sailing window, so give the shallow edges near the harbor margins a little extra room if you're still out toward dusk. Current in the harbor itself is gentle. The thing to note: the tide turns to **ebb (setting SSW, seaward) in the East Passage around 4 PM**, and with the wind blowing from the N against that outgoing flow, you can get a short, choppy "wind-against-tide" slop out in the Passage late afternoon. Nothing dangerous, but a casual crew shouldn't be surprised if it gets bouncy out there after 4 — another good reason to be heading back by then.

## Good to know
Dress for it: air temps only in the upper 40s and a brisk breeze mean it'll feel cold on the water — layers, foul-weather gear, hats and gloves are not overkill. Skies run cloudy to partly sunny with a **chance of a shower mainly in the morning**, so let the first showers pass and aim to launch late morning/midday. With gusts into the low-to-mid 20s (and the marine forecast flagging stronger gusts possible), this is a **life-jackets-on, maybe-tuck-a-reef** day, especially for smaller boats or lighter crews. A sensible plan: head out around **noon**, enjoy the steady breeze through the afternoon, and be back in by **4–4:30 PM** before the ebb chop builds and the light starts to fade.
