## The day
It's a good day to get out — think bright, blue-sky October sailing with a steady workhorse breeze rather than a lazy drifter. The wind is a puffy westerly: nice sailing most of the time, but with gusts running well above the average, so it leans toward the "spirited" end for a family daysail. Dress warm, keep a hand on the mainsheet in the puffs, and you'll have a lovely afternoon.

## Wind
Expect a **W to WNW** breeze all day. The forecast average sits around 7 knots, but that undersells it — the real anemometers up the bay are already reading closer to 10–12 knots (Conimicut Light had a steady 10 WNW at mid-morning), and gusts are nudging the high teens to 20. So plan for **nice, powered-up sailing with real puffs** — comfortable with a reef option in your back pocket if you're in a smaller or tippier boat. The breeze gradually veers (edges clockwise toward the NW) through the day. The best window is **late morning into early afternoon**, roughly 11:00–15:00, while the sun has the breeze lively. Classic soft spot: tuck in close under the **Poppasquash shore to the west** and you'll fall into its lee — the wind goes light and patchy there, then fills back in and veers as you clear the point, so don't be surprised by the lull.

## Tide & current
High water is at **10:51** (about 3.8 ft) and it drains to a **low at 16:25 — and it's a low one, just 0.5 ft**. Give the shallow harbor edges and the flats off Poppasquash a wide berth through mid-to-late afternoon so you don't find the bottom. Current is gentle inside the harbor itself. Out at the **harbor mouth by Hog Island** the flow turns to a seaward ebb (**setting SSW, down-bay**) from about 14:15; in the **East Passage** that ebb is stronger, near a knot. Since today's wind is westerly — mostly across the bay's current axis rather than against it — you shouldn't see nasty wind-against-tide chop, just a modest 2–3 ft on the open water toward the Passage. Nothing a casual sailor can't enjoy.

## Good to know
Gorgeous fall conditions: **mostly sunny**, highs around **75°F**, and only a slim **13% rain chance** — no thunderstorm worries. Mornings and the water are cooler (down to the mid-50s), so bring a layer and maybe foul-weather gear for spray. Because it's gusty with high-teens puffs, **life jackets on** is the smart call, especially for kids and dinghy sailors. Head out late morning to catch the best breeze over the high tide, and aim to be back in by **mid-afternoon (around 15:30–16:00)** — both to stay clear of that very low water and to wrap up while the breeze is still friendly.
