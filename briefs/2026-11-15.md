## The day
This is a bright, blustery November sail — sunny skies, a brisk northwesterly, and air in the mid-40s to low-50s. If you like a lively boat and you dress for the cold, it'll be a fun, spirited day. If you were hoping for a lazy family drift, this one is on the busy side: 10–15 kt with gusts near 20 means an active afternoon and a chilly ride, so pick your crew accordingly.

## Wind
The breeze is up and steady out of the **northwest** (around 315–330°) and holds that way all day — it won't fill or fade dramatically the way a summer sea breeze does. Expect a solid **10–15 kt with gusts to ~18–20**, so plenty of power; smaller boats and lighter crews should think about a **reef**. It's a classic gusty, shifty northerly coming off the land to the north, so trust your telltales and keep a hand on the sheet in the puffs. There's noticeably more punch out toward the **harbor mouth by Hog Island Shoal** — the live anemometer up-bay at Conimicut is already reading 16 kt gusting into the 20s, while it's a touch softer (9–10 kt) down toward Newport and the East Passage mouth. For a calmer first few tacks, stay tucked up inside the harbor; you'll also find a **soft, puffy patch in the lee to the east/southeast of Poppasquash Neck** where the wind spills off the point — nice for a breather, but the gusts snap back in once you clear its shadow. Best window is roughly **late morning through mid-afternoon** while the sun is up and the breeze is most consistent.

## Tide & current
**High water is at 11:32** (about 3.0 ft) and **low is at 17:11** (0.76 ft). That means the water drops steadily all afternoon, so by late day give the **shallow edges near the harbor shores and any thin spots** a wide berth — be mindful of your draft on the way back in. Current inside the harbor stays gentle (flood easing to slack around 1 PM, then a light seaward ebb building). Out in the **East Passage** the flow is stronger — the ebb picks up through the afternoon running down-bay to the SSW — so if you venture out past Hog Island, expect more current and a little more chop where that seaward flow meets the breeze. Nothing dramatic, just don't be surprised by some short, bumpy water out in the Passage.

## Good to know
Dress **warm and in layers** — it's a cold-weather sail with highs only in the upper 40s to low 50s, and that NW wind will feel sharp on the water even under full sun. Skies are **mostly sunny with essentially no rain** (2% chance) and no thunderstorm concern. Because it'll be **gusty into the high teens/low 20s, wear your life jackets** and keep an eye on everyone — cold water this time of year leaves no margin. Plan to **head out mid-morning** once you're bundled up and **be back by mid-afternoon**: daylight is short in November (sunset is around 4:30), and the water's dropping toward that late-afternoon low. Hot drinks and spare gloves are more than welcome on a day like this.
