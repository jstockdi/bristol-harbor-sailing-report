## The day
Yes — go sailing, just dress for October. It's a crisp, sunny day with a steady northerly in the 10–15 kt range and gusts into the high teens to low 20s, so this is a **spirited daysail** rather than a lazy drifter. Experienced crews will love it; if you've got kids or a nervous first-timer aboard, you'll want to keep a reef handy and stay tucked up inside the harbor where it's flatter. The breeze and sunshine are reliable all day, so the main thing to manage is the chill and the gusts, not finding wind.

## Wind
Breeze is **up and blowing from the N/NNE** right from the morning and holds all day — the live anemometers are already reading a solid 12 kt at Newport and nearly 15 kt up at Conimicut as the morning goes on, and that matches the forecast nicely (good confidence today). Expect a comfortable **"nicely powered-up" 10–13 kt** through most of the harbor, nudging a touch stronger (11–13 kt) out toward the harbor mouth and the East Passage, with **gusts to 19–20 kt** — and NWS is flagging afternoon puffs up toward 25, so reef if you're lightly crewed.

Because this is a land-driven northerly rather than a sea breeze, it'll feel **shifty and puffy** — the direction wanders and swings, so keep a hand on the sheet. The classic soft spot today sits in the **lee of Poppasquash Neck, down its E/SE side (the western shore)** — you'll sail into a quiet patch there and find the breeze again as you clear the point. The inner harbor also runs a notch lighter and more sheltered, which is exactly where to go if you want to take it easy.

**Best window:** late morning into early afternoon, before the afternoon gusts get their strongest. Everything's sailable all day, but that's the sweet spot for a relaxed outing.

## Tide & current
High water was early (~06:00), with a **very low tide around 12:02 (just 0.2 ft)** and the next high at 18:20. That midday low is worth respecting — **give the shallow edges and the shoals off Hog Island a wide berth around noon**, and don't cut corners near the harbor fringes.

Current is gentle inside the harbor and slack near the mouth around **11:30**, then the **flood builds through the afternoon** (setting NNE, up-bay into the harbor). Here's the heads-up: with the wind out of the N blowing *against* that afternoon flood, you'll get **wind-against-tide chop**, most noticeable out in the East Passage where the current runs harder. Nothing dangerous, but it'll make for a bumpier, wetter ride out past Hog Island in the afternoon — stay inside the harbor if you want it smoother.

## Good to know
Gorgeous but cool: **sunny and clear skies, no rain, highs only around 52–54°F** and dropping toward the upper 40s. Dress in real layers — foulies or a windbreaker, a hat, and gloves will all earn their keep once the wind-chill and spray factor in. With gusts into the 20s and chilly water, this is a **life-jackets-on day for everyone**, and a good day to clip in if you single-hand.

Simple plan: **head out mid-to-late morning** to catch the best of the breeze on a flatter tide, and aim to be **back in by mid-afternoon** before the gusts peak and the flood chop builds. Plenty of daylight and sunshine to enjoy — just keep it warm, keep it reefed if short-handed, and you'll have a terrific fall sail.
