## The day
It's a genuinely nice day to get out — just a proper late-autumn one, so dress for it. You've got a steady 8-knot breeze averaging through the window with gusts into the high teens and low 20s, bright skies, and almost no rain risk. That puts it in the "spirited, hands-on" bracket rather than a lazy drifter: fun and lively for anyone comfortable in a bit of breeze, a touch brisk for a mellow family potter. The main catch is the calendar — cold air and an early sunset mean this is a midday outing, not an all-day one.

## Wind
Expect a **SW breeze in the morning that veers around to NW through the afternoon** — the models and the live anemometers are in good agreement, so you can trust that shift. Out at the Passage mouth Newport is reading a gentle 6 or so knots of SW, but up-bay Conimicut is already pushing 16–18 from the NW, which is your clue to how the afternoon fills in: once it goes NW it's a **gradient breeze off the land — puffy and shifty**, so keep a hand on the sheet for the gusts to 20+. Figure **nice powered-up sailing** most of the window, edging toward "think about a reef or shortening sail" in the strongest NW puffs. The calmest patches will be tucked in close under the **Poppasquash shore on the west side**, which throws a lee to its east on a NW wind — handy if you want a gentler stretch, but you'll find the holes and soft spots there too. Best window is roughly **late morning into early afternoon**, before the chill and the fading light take over.

## Tide & current
High water was early at **06:01**, with a **low — and a notably low, below-datum one — at 12:08** right in the middle of your window, then the tide rebuilds to another high around **18:28**. With that deep midday low, **give the shallow edges and flats a wide berth**, especially the margins off Poppasquash and the inner-harbor shoals — there'll be less water than usual. Current-wise, the harbor mouth is **ebbing (setting SSW, seaward) through the morning to a slack around 11:15**, then **floods back up-bay (NNE) through the afternoon**. It stays gentle right in the harbor; the livelier flow (close to a knot) is out in the **East Passage** off Hog Island. Heads-up for the afternoon: the NW breeze working against that up-bay flood can kick up a **short, bumpy chop near the harbor mouth and in the Passage** — nothing alarming, just don't be surprised by it when you poke your nose out past Hog Island.

## Good to know
A cool, bright November day — highs around **60°F but dipping toward the mid-40s**, partly to mostly sunny, and essentially **no rain (3%)**. The real story is wind-chill: a NW breeze gusting to 20 over cold water feels a lot colder than the thermometer says, so **layer up, bring gloves and a warm hat, and plan for spray**. With those gusts, this is a **life-jackets-on day** for everyone aboard. Aim to **head out late morning** to catch the best of the light and breeze, and **be back by around 16:00** — sunset comes early this time of year and you don't want to be out in the cold after dark. Have fun — it'll be a brisk, rewarding sail.
