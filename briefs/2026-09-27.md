## The day
Honestly, this one's a judgment call. The breeze itself is perfectly sailable — a brisk, gusty northeasterly in the 10-knot range — but the sky is the problem: showers and thunderstorms are likely through the day, with rain chances up near 75%. On a settled day this would be a fun, spirited sail; with storms in the forecast it's a **marginal** day, and the safe play is a short morning outing (or a rain check). Keep one eye on the western horizon the whole time and don't get caught out if it darkens.

## Wind
Expect a **NE to NNE breeze** — it's coming off the land from the north, so it'll be **shifty and puffy** rather than steady. Averages sit around 9–10 kt, but the gusts tell the real story: they're pushing into the low-to-mid 20s, so plan for **powered-up sailing with real puffs** and be ready to ease or tuck in a reef when a dark line comes through. The live readings back this up — Conimicut up the bay is already blowing a solid 14 kt NNE this morning, while Newport down at the mouth is lighter at 6–7 kt, so the up-bay end has more punch. In a northerly like this you'll find a **soft, flukey patch tucked under the Poppasquash shore on the west side** (its lee sits to the E/SE) — nice for a mellow moment, but the breeze will fill back in and swing around as you clear it. The **best window is the morning**, before the afternoon storm energy builds.

## Tide & current
High water is early at **8:38 AM (4.3 ft)**, then it drops to a **low around 2:32 PM that goes slightly negative (about -0.1 ft)** before filling again to a high at 8:59 PM. That midday-to-mid-afternoon low means **thin water over the shallow edges and flats** — give the harbor margins and any skinny spots a wide berth from roughly noon through mid-afternoon. Current-wise, the ebb runs **seaward (SSW, down-bay)** through the late morning — strongest out in the East Passage — and since the wind is also blowing roughly down-bay, wind and tide are mostly working together, so you shouldn't hit nasty stacked-up chop during the daytime. Things go slack around 2–3 PM near the harbor mouth and out at Dyer Island.

## Good to know
It'll be **cool and damp** — highs only around 68°F, dipping to the low 60s, with a wet, showery sky. Bring **proper foul-weather gear and a warm layer**; this is not a T-shirt afternoon. The gusts alone are worth **wearing your life jacket**, and the thunderstorm risk makes it doubly important — thunderstorms mean lightning, so if you hear thunder or see a dark, building cloud line to the west, **head in immediately and don't wait**. If you do go, **get out early (mid-to-late morning) and plan to be back at the dock by early afternoon**, well before the storms are most likely to fire. When in doubt today, there's no shame in postponing.
