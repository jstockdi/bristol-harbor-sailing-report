## The day
This is a lazy, sunny summer daysail — the kind of day for easy cruising, teaching, or just floating around with lunch aboard. Winds stay light all day (mostly 4–6 knots with gentle gusts to the low teens), so nobody's getting overpowered. If you love a spirited rail-down sail, this one may frustrate you; but for a calm family outing under blue skies and comfortable temperatures, it's about as pleasant as it gets. Bring a paddle or patience for the soft patches.

## Wind
The morning starts light and northerly (NNE), which is the overnight land breeze hanging on — expect drifter conditions early, honestly not worth rushing out before late morning. Through midday the breeze goes soft and swings around, and by early-to-mid afternoon the classic **SW sea breeze** should fill in from the south up the East Passage, settling into a southerly of roughly 5–6 knots. That's your best window: **about 1:00–5:00 PM**, when the breeze is steadiest and coming off the water. You'll find a touch more pressure to the **south and seaward — down toward Hog Island and the East Passage** — than up inside the harbor, so if it feels dead up top, reaching south often finds another knot or two. Watch for the usual **soft, sheltered patch tucked in behind Poppasquash Neck (the western shore)** — the breeze goes light and shifty in its lee before filling back in once you clear it. One note: the live buoys this morning are reading a bit lighter than the models predicted, so treat the numbers as an optimistic ceiling — plan for the lighter end.

## Tide & current
High water is at **10:26 AM** (about 4.5 ft) and low is at **4:05 PM** (basically dead low, near 0 ft) — so the afternoon has the thinnest water of the day. If you're poking around the shallow harbor edges or shoal spots near Hog Island Shoal, give them extra room through mid-afternoon so you don't find the bottom. Current is gentle at the harbor mouth (slack around **11:42 AM**, then a light seaward ebb building through the afternoon), so nothing dramatic in the harbor itself. The **East Passage runs harder** — over a knot of ebb setting SSW/seaward by early-to-mid afternoon. With the sea breeze coming from the south against that outgoing tide, you can get a little short, bouncy chop out in the Passage; nothing scary in this light air, just don't be surprised by the slop if you venture out past Hog Island.

## Good to know
Gorgeous conditions overall — sunny to partly cloudy, highs around **77°F**, and essentially **no rain** (2% chance, no thunderstorms). It'll be warm and bright, so pack sunscreen, water, and a hat; the light wind means the sun does the talking. No reef needed and no gusty drama, but always have life jackets aboard and handy. A good plan: **head out around late morning to midday**, be settled in for the best breeze from about 1:00 PM, and aim to be back before evening as the wind fades again. An easy, no-stress day on the water.
