## The day
This is a mellow one. Winds are light across the board — averaging only 4–5 knots from the harbor out past Hog Island — so think easy, unhurried sailing rather than a spirited rail-down afternoon. If you enjoy quiet ghosting, a light boat, or just pottering around the harbor with the family, it's a pleasant day to be on the water. If you want power and spray, this probably isn't your day. The good news: the models are in near-total agreement on the light forecast, so there are no nasty surprises lurking.

## Wind
Expect a light **northwesterly (around 300°)** through the morning, gradually swinging around toward the **south** as the afternoon wears on. Strengths sit firmly in **drifter-to-light-cruising** territory — roughly 4–5 knots average with the odd puff into the low teens if a cloud edge kicks something through. The live picture backs this up: Newport at the Passage mouth is reading a soft 3–4 knots, while Conimicut up-bay is a touch friskier near 8–9 — a reminder that the breeze is patchy and you'll want to hunt for the darker water. Your best window is roughly **late morning into early afternoon**, when there's the most to work with. Expect genuine soft spots tucked in behind **Poppasquash Neck** on the west shore — that shoreline throws a lee in a NW breeze, so you may drift there before finding air again as you clear out toward open water. A little more consistency lives out toward the **harbor mouth and Hog Island Shoal** if you're chasing pressure.

## Tide & current
**High water is at 10:06 AM (4.45 ft)** and it drains to a very low **low at 3:57 PM (basically 0.0 ft)** before filling again toward the evening. That afternoon low is worth respecting — give the shallow harbor edges, flats, and any thin water near the shorelines a wide berth from about 2–5 PM. Current is gentle at the harbor mouth (near slack around 11 AM, a light SSW ebb building through early afternoon), but out in the **East Passage** the ebb has more teeth — up to about 1.25 knots setting **seaward (SSW)** around midday. With the light NW breeze nudging against that ebb you might feel a little short chop out past Hog Island, but at these wind speeds it'll be mild — nothing to trouble a casual sailor.

## Good to know
Cool and comfortable — highs only around **64–68°F** under a mix of partly-to-mostly sunny skies, with essentially **no rain risk (3%)** and no thunderstorm concerns. It's late-September sailing, so bring a layer; even in sun it'll feel crisp once you're moving, especially in the morning. Sun protection is still worth it on the brighter stretches. A good plan is to **head out mid-to-late morning** to catch the best of the breeze near the midday window, and aim to be **back by mid-afternoon** — both because the wind tends to fade and because that big afternoon low water makes the shallow edges unforgiving. With winds this light there's no need for gusty-weather caution, but as always, life jackets on and a paddle or motor aboard in case you find yourself parked in a soft patch.
