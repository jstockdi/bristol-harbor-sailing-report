## The day
This is a mellow, take-it-easy kind of day — a fine one for a relaxed family sail rather than a thrilling rail-down romp. The breeze is on the light side (mostly 4–6 knots), the water will be calm, and it swings around from a morning northerly to the classic afternoon southerly off the bay. The main thing to watch isn't the wind but the sky: there's a real chance of patchy fog and rain showers rolling through, so treat it as a "go when the window looks good" day rather than an all-day plan.

## Wind
Expect a soft, drifter-ish start in the morning, coming out of the NNE. As the afternoon warms, the sea breeze fills in from the S/SSW up the East Passage and around Hog Island — a gentle 5–6 knots, with the odd puff into the low teens toward the mouth and seaward. This is easy, pleasant sailing wind: enough to move nicely, never enough to overpower you. The live anemometers at Newport and Conimicut are reading right in line with the forecast (around 4–6 knots, already backing into the south), so confidence is good on the light-air story.

Your best window is early-to-mid afternoon, once the southerly settles in — that's when you'll get the steadiest breeze, and it's a touch stronger the farther out toward Hog Island and the East Passage you go. Expect a soft, flukey patch tucked in behind the Poppasquash shore (the west side of the harbor) when the wind is out of the south; if you glide into a hole there, nudge back out toward open water to find the pressure again.

## Tide & current
High water is around 10:30 AM and again late tonight; low water is about 4:13 PM (a modest 0.2 ft). With that afternoon low, give the shallow edges near the harbor fringes and any thin spots a little extra room — nothing dramatic, just don't cut the corners at the margins.

Current is light and friendly inside the harbor. At the harbor mouth by Hog Island it goes slack around 11:48 AM, then ebbs (running seaward, SSW) through the afternoon, peaking near 2 PM before easing to slack again about 3:47 PM. Out in the East Passage the flow is stronger — roughly a knot on the ebb — so if you wander out there mid-afternoon you'll feel it pushing you seaward. With such light wind against that ebb you might notice a bit of gentle chop or a "standing still" feeling near the mouth, but nothing a casual sailor needs to worry about.

## Good to know
Comfortable temperatures, 69–77°F, so dress light but bring a layer in case fog and cloud keep the sun off — skies run from partly sunny to mostly cloudy with patchy fog. The catch is a fairly high shower chance (up to ~77%), so pack a rain layer and keep an eye upwind; if you see a wall of rain or fog closing in, head back early — visibility can drop fast in fog. Because the wind stays light there's no need to fuss over reefing, but everyone should still have life jackets on hand as always. A nice plan: head out around lunchtime as the southerly fills, enjoy the early-to-mid afternoon, and aim to be back at the dock by mid-to-late afternoon before the shower risk peaks and the light fades from the breeze.
