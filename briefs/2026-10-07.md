## Wind

A classic autumn SSW evening breeze, **206–214°** and gently veering (~8°) through the window — nothing dramatic, just a slow clockwise drift toward the SW. The strength story is a **north–south pressure gradient with a hole in the middle**: more breeze off **Poppasquash Point (13.2 kt)** and out in the **East Passage (12.6 kt)**, a healthy **11 kt inside the inner harbor**, but a pronounced **soft patch right across the Hog Island mouth and just S of the island (7–7.5 kt)** — the island's own lee on this SSW flow. Gusts run to **~21–22 kt** everywhere, so the puffs reach down even into the light band.

Live obs back the stronger regime: **Newport** at the Passage mouth is averaging **13.9 kt SSW** (peak ~16), and **Conimicut Light** up-bay is honking **18.1 kt SSW** (peak 20). The model (`wind_check` ratio 1.1, "obs and model broadly agree") and a tight inner-harbor spread (0.8 kt) give good confidence on direction and the mid-teens pressure where the course is exposed; just respect that the Hog Island soft zone is real, not a model artifact. NWS has SW 10–15 kt gusting 25.

## Current

Both stations are near the turn during the window, so current is a **second-order factor early and a growing one late**.

- **Harbor mouth (Hog Island NW):** a weak **flood (~0.36 kt, set ~011° NNE up-bay/into the harbor)** is already fading; **slack at 19:16**, then a light ebb begins building toward the end of the window — set swinging to **~199° SSW, seaward**.
- **East Passage (Dyer Island W):** stronger channel axis, slightly ahead in phase. Flood fading to **slack at 18:53**, then **ebb building (set ~216° SSW)** and ramping hard after the finish (near **1 kt by 21:11**).

Net: for most of 18:00–19:15 you've got near-slack to a dribble of up-bay flood; after ~19:15–19:30 the whole system tips to a **seaward SSW ebb**, felt first and strongest in the East Passage channel.

## Tactical call

**Course-agnostic:**
- **Chase the pressure, dodge the Hog Island hole.** The breeze is honest in the mid-teens off **Poppasquash Point** and out in the **East Passage**, but drops to single digits right across the **Hog Island mouth / just S of the island**. Don't get parked crossing that band — if you must transit it, do it on a puff and keep speed up.
- **Current is a late-window lever.** Through the first half it's near-slack at the mouth and in the Passage, so tactics are breeze-first. After ~19:15–19:30 the **ebb builds SSW/seaward** — strongest in the East Passage channel. If you're working up-bay (N) late, **stay off the mid-channel SSW set and favor the shallower shore edges**; if you're running seaward, ride the channel ebb.
- The veer is small (~8°), so play oscillations in phase rather than banking one side.

**If the course is set:**
- **N–S windward/leeward:** the **E–W pressure gradient is the lever** — the Poppasquash (west) edge and seaward end carry more breeze than the Hog mouth; late in the window a building SSW ebb nudges the up-bay beat toward the shore lanes.
- **J-hook out of the harbor:** start in near-slack inner-harbor water (solid 11 kt), then expect the **soft, confused band at the mouth** before you reach the stronger Passage breeze — and after ~19:15 the mouth begins to ebb against you on the way out.
- **Around Hog Island:** budget for the **light lee on the S/SW side of the island** and near-slack-to-weak current early; the leg timing matters, as the Passage tips to a firm SSW ebb by the back half.
- **Out-and-back (light air):** pure **pressure play** — get out to the East Passage breeze and back; current is minor until the final reach home, when the seaward ebb starts to assist.
