## The day
This is a lovely late-season day to get out — bundle up and go. A steady west-southwest breeze in the low teens gives you a proper, spirited sail without ever tipping into "too much," and the sky is sunny and dry all day. It's not a lazy drifter and it's not a handful; it's the kind of day where the boat moves nicely and you'll want a warm layer and gloves more than you'll want more wind. Call it a crisp, satisfying November sail.

## Wind
The breeze is already in and settled: live anemometers at Newport (the East Passage mouth) and Conimicut Light up the bay are both reading WSW around 9–11 kt with gusts into the low teens, and the forecast and live obs are in close agreement — so you can trust this one. Expect **WSW 8–12 kt steady, gusting into the mid-to-upper teens** through the afternoon, backing just slightly toward the SW as the day goes on. That's a nice, powered-up sail for most boats — nothing you'll need to reef, but enough that a gust will lean you over, so keep a hand on the sheet.

There's a touch more pressure out toward the open water off Poppasquash and down in the East Passage than tucked up in the inner harbor. The one soft spot to expect: in a westerly like this, the land behind **Poppasquash Neck (the west shore)** throws a lee to its east — so if you sail in close under that shore you'll find a lighter, puffier patch and the breeze will fill back in as you work back out toward open water. Best window is simply late morning right on through the afternoon; it holds steady the whole time.

## Tide & current
High water was around **8:04 AM**, and a **very low tide bottoms out at 1:47 PM (just 0.2 ft)** before filling again toward the evening high at 8:24 PM. That early-afternoon low is worth a thought — give the shallow edges near the harbor's shoreline and any thin spots around the flats a wide berth around midday, especially if you draw more than a couple of feet.

Current is gentle inside the harbor. Out at the harbor mouth by Hog Island it's ebbing (running SSW, seaward) through late morning, goes slack around **1:30 PM**, then turns to flood and builds up-bay into the evening. Out in the East Passage the flow is a bit stronger, slack there around **2:40 PM**. With a WSW wind and a seaward-running ebb earlier in the day, you may notice a little short chop near the harbor mouth and off the island tips — nothing dramatic at these wind speeds, just a reminder it'll be bumpier out in the gut than it is tucked inside.

## Good to know
It's a bright, dry day — partly to mostly sunny, essentially no rain chance (1%). The catch is the cold: air temps only reach the **low-to-mid 50s°F**, and with a steady breeze and cold November water it'll feel colder on the water than on shore. Dress in real layers, bring a hat and gloves, and a splash layer isn't a bad idea near the mouth. Because the gusts reach the upper teens, **wear your life jacket** — easy to do when you're already layered up, and smart in cold water. Head out late morning to catch the steady breeze, and plan to be back in before the light starts fading in late afternoon (sunset comes early this time of year). Enjoy it — crisp, sunny, steady wind; a classic fall sail.
